## CRITIQUE
VERDICT: Mostly sound, with fixable problems. I spot-checked about 20 load-bearing citations with WebFetch and most numbers trace exactly to their sources: Raptive (n=3,000, nearly 50%, -14%), CMI (95/89/87/58/39%, and 24% for differentiation), Orbit (13.9%, AI users and non-users equally likely to report strong results), Krebs (1,590 sites, 22%/32%/46%), BetterUp (n=1,004, 40%, 15.4%, 1h51m, $186, 54% vs 38.5%), the BetterUp 2026 follow-up (~2,300; 44%/55% Canadian), Russell et al. (1 of 300, 53.1%/35.9%), UX Tools (52.2/34.2/1.4%), Faros (+154/+91/+9%), Stack Overflow (66%, 45.2%, 3.1%), Anthropic's harness post, SlopShape (97.0/96.1), Meincke (37 of 45), Figma 2025 and 2026, IAB (82 vs 45%), Billion Dollar Boy (60 to 26%), Chakrabarty CHI (28/20/18/17%, 18% deletions), Lovable ($400M in Feb 2026) and Menlo coding/horizontal spend.

The problems are mostly in interpretation, not fabrication:
- **Watanabe 45.1%.** The paper finds agent PRs need no more revision than human PRs, so this is not an AI-specific pain, and it is used as a success baseline.
- **Wetch's 21/28.** Claude Opus 4.5 judged Claude outputs, which breaks the synthesis's own critic rules.
- **Zhou & Lee.** This is an art-platform study cited as UI evidence.
- **Hintze.** This is an iterative image-caption loop, extended to single-shot ad generation.

**Article fidelity.** The source article does exist: Lenny's Newsletter, Sep 1, 2026, public portion fetched. It puts T1 and T2 in Discover, the critic loop plus image and video generation (T3/T4/T5) in Define, and T6 onward in Deliver. Studio Build relabels these phases. It also adds a 'keep structure conventional' rule that contradicts the article's own T2 examples (asymmetric layouts, isometric cities, pixel art).

**Ranking.** The top three are defensible, but there is no explicit rubric:
- Workslop at #2 is the weakest fit to the bell-curve thesis. It is about missing substance, not sameness; three blocks sit out; and part of its pain is accuracy, the same reason deep research was rejected.
- Ad creative at #6 sits below decks at #4 even though its audience-side evidence is stronger.
- The platform guardrail at #8 is a distribution channel, not a use case.

**Configurations.** Six are genuinely distinct wirings. Reader-Ready Document and Seeded Angle to Owned Voice share the same polish sequence (T6, T7, T3, T8) and read as one configuration with knobs. The order of T6 and T3 is inconsistent across configurations and breaks the synthesis's own 'T6 after any critic loop' insight. Lean Diff Gate and Headless Platform Guardrail drift away from the article's distinctiveness thesis.

**Missing use case.** The largest gap is standalone image, illustration and brand-identity generation, where the strongest novelty-decline evidence (Zhou & Lee) actually belongs.

CONFIG SET: Eight configurations; six are genuinely distinct wirings:
- **Studio Build:** full chassis with a parallel seeded front-end, a nested specialist asset sub-pipeline, and a pixel-judging critic loop.
- **Divergence Engine:** wide parallel fan-out ending in a decision, with T3 used as a ranking panel.
- **Lean Diff Gate:** a single-artifact gate on objective signals, with subtraction on both sides of the critic.
- **Media Pipeline:** chained specialist models with state handoff, per-shot audits, and live A/B testing as the outer critic.
- **Human-Led Sounding Board:** inverted authorship, where the human drafts and the blocks act as parallel auditors.
- **Headless Platform Guardrail:** best-of-N selection with diversity enforced across projects and no per-artifact human.

The nesting of Divergence Engine into other configurations is a good modular design choice.

**Near-duplicate.** Reader-Ready Document and Seeded Angle to Owned Voice share the same polish sequence (T6, T7, blind T3, T8) and differ only in parameters: a nested divergence front-end, how heavy T8 is, and the critic's reference set. Merge them, or make Seeded Angle's cross-series outer loop its defining topology.

**Inconsistent ordering.** T6 vs T3 order varies without a stated principle, and two text configurations break the synthesis's own 'T6 after critic loops' insight.

**Design configuration.** It is included and largely faithful at the block level:
- Seed strings come from a script.
- The T2 sequence is broad list, then human taste, then AI-written brief.
- A stronger critic judges screenshots and iterates to a threshold.
- Image and video generation are routed to specialist models.
- T6, T7 and T8 finish the work.

But it diverges from the verified article in three ways:
- It relabels the phases. The article puts T3/T4/T5 in Define, not Deliver.
- It adds a 'conventional structure' constraint that contradicts the article's T2 examples (asymmetric layouts).
- It runs an in-loop T7 scan that risks recreating the up-front ban effect the article warns against.

The final T3 regression check and the critic's flaw taxonomy are reasonable additions but should be labelled as amendments. Bundling deck mode and brand mode into Studio Build is acceptable, but brand mode removes the Discover phase entirely and is really a different wiring.

**Sit-out choices.**
- Sensible: T1 out for signal-bearing documents and routine code, T5 out for text work, T8 reduced to flagging placeholder copy in the headless configuration.
- Questionable: T7 out for teaching (template sameness is a structural tell), and T4 out for documents and code, which relies on a narrower T4 definition than other use cases use.

**Drift from the article.** Lean Diff Gate (convergence-seeking, correctness-dominated) and Headless Platform Guardrail (no human taste, T3 turned into a selector) are useful but drift furthest from the article's thesis. Label them adjacent, or move Headless Platform Guardrail to more_to_explore or a distribution-channel note.
