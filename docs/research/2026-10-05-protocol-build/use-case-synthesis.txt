### #1 Bylined and brand written content: thought leadership, newsletters, blogs, LinkedIn/social posts, ghostwritten executive posts, marketing copy
PERSONA: Content marketers, brand and comms teams, newsletter writers, founders and executives who post under their own name, ghostwriters and social media managers. 95% of B2B marketers use AI, and 89% use it for content creation (CMI 2026).
CORE_PAIN: AI drafts converge on the same angles, structures and vocabulary across writers and across models. Practised readers recognise the tells, and platforms now flag and down-rank them. Suspected AI authorship roughly halves trust and weakens the parasocial bond. Results have not improved even though adoption is near-universal.
EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: Strong. The base is peer-reviewed reader experiments (ACL 2025, JCR 2026, JBR 2025, CHI 2025, Science Advances), large industry surveys (CMI n=1,015; Raptive n=3,000; Orbit n=1,042) and observed platform behaviour (LinkedIn's slop flag). Prevalence figures come from detector-based vendor estimates and should be treated as directional.
MARKET_REALITY: Enterprise marketing AI spend was $660M in 2025 (Menlo). Generic copy has been commoditised: Jasper survived by pivoting to brand-voice governance. Incumbents sell grounding and compliance (brand voice, style guides, Markup AI content guardians) but nothing that sets a bar for excellence or distinctiveness. Grassroots demand for after-the-fact tell removal is very large (humanizer skill 54.2k stars; humanizer sites about 21M monthly visits, much of it detector evasion). 49% of US consumers say GenAI has made content worse, and 50% prefer brands that avoid it (Gartner 2026). Demand is pulled by the writer, because the writer's own audience applies the penalty.
FREQUENCY_AND_STAKES: Production runs weekly to daily for most marketers and creators. The stakes are cumulative: brand trust, reach (platform down-ranking), pipeline and personal reputation. One flagged post costs little, but a feed that reads as AI erodes the asset over months.
WHY_PROTOCOL_FITS: The three measured gaps map almost one-to-one onto the blocks: convergence across writers and models (T1/T2), recognisable tells at both word and structure level (T7), and the authenticity penalty (T8). Expert repair is largely subtraction (T6). Naive judges fail on prose, so T3 has to rank against a reference corpus, which is what the protocol prescribes. Rank rationale: this case has the strongest combination of directly felt pain, very high frequency, strong reader-side evidence and near-complete block fit. It ranks above workslop because the writer, not a third party, pays the penalty, so the demand is real.
RENDERED_VIEW: The piece as the audience meets it. For social, that is the feed card with the hook above the fold, then the expanded post with its image. For long-form, it is the published page or email render (headline, dek, body, images). The critic sees it shuffled among 5-10 pieces made up of the author's own best past work and category leaders. It gets no prompt, outline or author notes.
HUMAN_VOICE_SURFACES: Headline, hook or lede, thesis sentence, personal anecdotes and examples, transitions, closing line or kicker, and CTA. For short social posts, at minimum the hook and last line. For emotional or relational posts, the whole post.
EVIDENCE:
  - Five frequent LLM users together misclassified 1 of 300 articles and beat detectors even on paraphrased or humanized text. Their cues were vocabulary (53%), structure (36%) and lack of originality — Russell, Karpinska & Iyyer, ACL 2025 — https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15654v2
  - Suspected AI content cut reader trust by nearly 50% and cut purchase consideration for adjacent ads by 14%. The penalty also hit human text that readers believed was AI — Raptive (n=3,000) via PPC Land — https://ppc.land/raptive-study-shows-ai-content-cuts-reader-trust-by-half/
  - 95% of B2B marketers use AI and 87% report productivity gains, but only 39% report better content performance. 24% name differentiation as a top challenge — CMI 2026 (n=1,015) — https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/articles/b2b-content-marketing-trends-research/
  - AI users and non-users report 'strong results' at the same rate (about 14%), the lowest in 12 years — Orbit Media 2026 (n=1,042) — https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/blogging-statistics/
  - Across 8,035 professional edits of LLM paragraphs: awkward word choice 28%, unnecessary exposition 18%, cliches 17%. 18% of edits were pure deletions, and no model family did better than the others — Chakrabarty, Laban & Wu, CHI 2025 — https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509v1
  - AI disclosure on TikTok reduces engagement by weakening the parasocial connection, which runs through perceived creator effort — Carney et al., JCR 2026 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/3bbe7da7a0c9544fbcd9816fe56fd540/
  - Each extra GPT-4 essay adds fewer new ideas than each extra human essay, and the gap survives prompt and parameter tweaks — Moon et al. 2025 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/a5b284839107586aad3281027dafa51b/?utm_source=claude_desktop
  - Most LLMs barely beat random at judging writing quality. Generate-and-rank with a reward model trained on expert edits won 66% expert preference — Chakrabarty et al., WQ/WQRM — https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07532
  - LinkedIn shipped a 'Seems like AI slop' flag (Jul 2026). A vendor detector rated 81.2% of 5,000 long posts as likely AI — Originality.ai — https://originality.ai/blog/ai-content-published-linkedin
CENTRAL: T1 (seeded angles and hooks across a content series, not seeded prose); T2 (human picks the thesis from their own experience before drafting); T6; T7 (word- and structure-level catalog, versioned); T3 (ranked against the author's best work plus category leaders); T8 (heavy)
OPTIONAL: T4 (header images, carousels, original diagrams; real photos often carry more authenticity than generated ones)
SIT OUT: T5: Text-first work. Repurposing into video is a separate media job and belongs in the Media Pipeline configuration.
RISKS:
  - Drifting into detector evasion: much of the humanizer market is evasion. T7 and T8 must be framed as quality and ownership, and required disclosures must stay intact.
  - Without a reference corpus, T3 rubber-stamps, because LLM judges are near-random on prose quality.
  - Removing surface tells leaves the structure intact (SlopShape still separates AI posts at 96% after rewording), so T7 must cover structure and run with T6 and T2.
  - The tell catalog drifts as models change and as humans adopt AI vocabulary.
  - Engagement metrics are noisy and slow, so success needs a pre-protocol baseline.
SUCCESS:
  - In blind tests, frequent-AI-user readers flag the piece no more often than human-written controls.
  - The blind critic ranks the piece in the top third of a lineup of the author's best work plus category leaders.
  - Embedding similarity across a quarter's pieces is at or below the author's pre-AI baseline.
  - Engagement or subscriptions beat the AI-first-draft baseline, and no 'seems like AI slop' flags appear.
  - The final piece is shorter than the first AI draft, and production time stays well below fully manual.

### #2 Internal workplace documents ('workslop'): memos, reports, PRDs, status updates, strategy docs, RCAs
PERSONA: Desk-based knowledge workers (managers, PMs, analysts, consultants, HR) who draft with copilots, and the colleagues who receive the output. Managers receive the most (54% vs 38.5% of individual contributors). HR has the heaviest rework in Workday's data.
CORE_PAIN: Documents look polished but carry no substance, so the work shifts to the recipient at about 2 hours per instance. They are padded and they cost the sender trust. Senders skip self-review and pass output on without checking it.
EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: Strong. Multiple large surveys: BetterUp/Stanford (n of roughly 1,000-1,150, plus a 2026 follow-up of about 2,300), Workday (n=3,200), KPMG/Melbourne (n>48,000) and Microsoft WTI (n=20,000). Preregistered reputation experiments: PNAS N=4,439 and Cardon n of about 1,100. Caveats: the HBR article is paywalled, and Workday's figures come via trade press.
MARKET_REALITY: Horizontal copilots are the largest enterprise GenAI app category: $7.2B of $8.4B horizontal spend in 2025 (Menlo). Nobody sells an 'anti-workslop' product. Today's fixes are norms (Shopify's 'slop grenades' memo), training, and recipients rewriting. The quantified cost (about $9M a year per 10,000 employees) gives buyers an ROI frame, but the buyer is the organisation (IT, L&D, leadership), not the sender. 86% treat AI output as a starting point, and 50% name quality control as a critical skill (Microsoft WTI 2026).
FREQUENCY_AND_STAKES: Daily, and the highest volume of any use case. Per-instance stakes are low to moderate (hours lost, a decision delayed). Cumulative stakes are high: team trust, a 37% loss of time savings to rework, and the sender's reputation.
WHY_PROTOCOL_FITS: Workslop is a missing thesis plus padding plus skipped review. A light T2 forces the sender's thinking before drafting. T6 attacks the length bias trained into models. T3 replaces the self-review that 66% skip with a simulated recipient. T7 and T8 protect the sender's credibility. T1, T4 and T5 add nothing. Rank rationale: largest volume and best-quantified cost, but the sender doesn't feel the pain, and the deepest cause (no thinking) is only partly fixable by a pipeline.
RENDERED_VIEW: The document exactly as the recipient opens it (email body or doc page with title, formatting and attachment list), plus one line of reader context, such as 'you are the director who must approve X by Friday'. No prompt, drafting notes or sender rationale.
HUMAN_VOICE_SURFACES: The bottom line or recommendation in the first lines, the explicit ask, any judgment call or opinion, and the sign-off. For manager-to-team or relational messages, the whole message.
EVIDENCE:
  - 40% received workslop in the past month. It is about 15.4% of work received, takes about 1h51m per instance and costs about $186 per employee per month — BetterUp Labs / Stanford Social Media Lab (Sep 2025) — https://www.betterup.com/blog/hidden-costs-workslop
  - 2026 follow-up (US/CA/UK, about 2,300 workers): 44% of Canadian desk workers received workslop and 55% admit sending it; about 2 hours per fix — https://www.betterup.com/blog/workslop-canada
  - 37% of the time AI saves is lost to rework, and only 14% consistently get net-positive outcomes — Workday via ERP Today — https://erp.today/workday-research-finds-ai-productivity-gains-are-lost-to-rework/
  - 66% rely on AI output without evaluating its accuracy, and 56% have made mistakes because of AI — KPMG / University of Melbourne (n>48,000) — https://kpmg.com/za/en/insights/2025/03/trust-attitudes-and-use-of-ai.html
  - Across 4 preregistered experiments (N=4,439), AI users were rated lazier and less competent — Reif, Larrick & Soll, PNAS 2025 — https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/duke-fuqua-insights/Is-AI-Damaging-Your-Professional-Image
  - Supervisors were seen as sincere by about 83% at low AI assistance versus 40-52% at high assistance — Cardon & Coman 2025 — https://news.ufl.edu/2025/08/writing-ai-work/
  - RLHF reward gains are largely driven by response length, so padding is trained in — Singhal et al. 2023 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03716
  - Practitioners report AI RCAs getting longer and less accurate, and a 50-page AI architecture doc that could have been 3 pages — Hacker News (anecdotal) — https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45337253
CENTRAL: T2 (light: the human states the decision, ask and single point before drafting); T6; T7; T3 (blind simulated recipient); T8 (voice surfaces only)
OPTIONAL: T1 (only when the document proposes options; nest the Divergence Engine)
SIT OUT: T4: Text artifacts. Any charts must come from data, not image models.; T5: No video in internal documents.
RISKS:
  - Senders don't feel the pain (55% admit sending workslop), so a multi-step protocol will only be used if it takes minutes or is built into the copilot or org norms.
  - Substance can't be manufactured. Without a human thesis the pipeline just produces shorter workslop.
  - Factual errors (56% made AI-caused mistakes) are invisible to a blind critic and need verification outside the blocks.
  - A nitpicking critic and over-verification can make documents longer and slower, so loops need a cap and findings must be scoped to actionability.
SUCCESS:
  - Recipient time-to-action and follow-up questions per document go down.
  - The share of received work rated as workslop falls below the roughly 15% baseline in team pulse surveys.
  - Documents are shorter than the AI first draft, with the decision or ask in the first two lines.
  - The blind recipient critic ranks the document at or above exemplary internal memos.
  - Sender trust and competence ratings match fully human-written controls.

### #3 AI-built interfaces: landing pages, marketing sites, MVP and product UI, prototypes (the article's own use case)
PERSONA: Non-designer founders, indie hackers and PMs using Lovable, v0, Bolt, Replit, Claude Code or Cursor. Product designers and design engineers exploring directions. Growth teams shipping pages. Builders inside companies working under an existing design system (brand-anchored mode).
CORE_PAIN: Output converges on one recognisable AI look: Inter or Space Grotesk, indigo gradients, a centred hero with a badge above the H1, icon-card grids, stock shadcn. Banning defaults just produces the next defaults. Agents praise their own mediocre visual work. Code-drawn gradients and blobs read as low effort. Designers heavily edit first drafts.
EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: Moderate to strong. Strong: designer surveys (UX Tools n=1,478; Figma n=2,500), large-scale evidence that novelty declines after generative tools (4M artworks), and evidence that general vision models are weak UI critics (UIClip). Moderate: an automated scan of 1,590 Show HN pages, first-party Anthropic statements, and the Design Theater benchmark. Gap: no A/B or conversion evidence that a distinctive page beats a generic one.
MARKET_REALITY: The fastest-growing market in the sweeps: Lovable at about $400M ARR (Feb 2026), about 8M users and 100k products a day; Replit at $525M annualized; v0 at 4M users; Claude Design launched for non-designers. Incumbents answer with grounding (design-system ingestion in Lovable, Figma Make and Claude Design), which buys consistency, not distinctiveness. Anti-slop demand lives in open-source skills and scanners: Impeccable 77.1k stars, Anthropic frontend-design, Hallmark 29.7k, Slop Cop. Enterprise design AI spend ($490M) is far below coding ($4.0B). 90% of builders say design is at least as important as before AI (Figma 2026).
FREQUENCY_AND_STAKES: Very high frequency at platform scale, with pages iterated often. The stakes are first-impression credibility ('design look' was the most-cited credibility factor, at 46.1%, Fogg 2002), being dismissed on sight (anecdotal), and brand erosion inside companies.
WHY_PROTOCOL_FITS: Every block has documented work to do here. T1 for convergence. T2 for the missing design vocabulary and vision. T3 because self-review fails on visuals. T4 for code-drawn assets. T5, optionally, for hero motion. T6 for additive clutter. T7 because a mature, evolving tell catalog exists. T8 for interchangeable headline copy. Rank rationale: best block fit and strongest market momentum, and it is the article's own case. It sits below the two writing cases because the commercial cost of the AI look has not been measured (no conversion evidence).
RENDERED_VIEW: Full-page screenshots at desktop (about 1440px) and mobile (about 390px) widths, plus an above-the-fold crop and a short screen recording when motion matters. These are shown in a shuffled lineup with 5-10 professional sites from the same category, or canonical brand screens in brand-anchored mode. No code, brief, design rationale or prior critiques.
HUMAN_VOICE_SURFACES: Hero headline and subhead, primary CTA labels, benefit and feature lines, the founder or 'about' story, and brand-bearing microcopy such as empty states and error messages.
EVIDENCE:
  - 22% of 1,590 Show HN sites showed 4 or more of 16 AI design patterns, and 32% showed 2-3 — Adrian Krebs (Apr 2026) — https://adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/
  - Anthropic attributes the Inter / purple-gradient look to 'distributional convergence' and notes that avoid-lists move the model to other defaults — Claude blog (Nov 2025) — https://www.claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills
  - The official frontend-design skill now lists second-order defaults to avoid: cream with serif and terracotta, near-black with acid green, the SaaS card kit — anthropics SKILL.md — https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/claude-code/main/plugins/frontend-design/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md
  - Agents 'confidently' praise mediocre design. A separate skeptical evaluator working from Playwright screenshots over 5-15 iterations was far more tractable — Anthropic Engineering (Mar 2026) — https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps
  - 52.2% of designers cite AI output quality as a blocker, 34.2% heavily edit first drafts, and 1.4% trust AI without oversight — UX Tools Spring 2026 (n=1,478) — https://www.uxtools.co/survey/2026/state-of-prototyping
  - 54% of designers vs 68% of developers say AI improves quality, and only 32% can rely on its output — Figma 2025 AI report (n=2,500) — https://www.figma.com/blog/figma-2025-ai-report-perspectives/
  - After text-to-image adoption, favourites per view rose but both average and maximum visual novelty fell — Zhou & Lee, PNAS Nexus 2024 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10914360
  - GPT-4V judged UI quality at 51.6% (near chance), versus 75.1% for a specialised critic — UIClip, UIST 2024 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12500
  - About 1 in 4 stated design rationales were not implemented across 120 generated interfaces, so a critic that reads rationale is misled — Design Theater (Jul 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22928
  - Users prefer simple, prototypical layouts, and unfamiliar designs look less attractive — Tuch et al., via Google Research — https://research.google/blog/users-love-simple-and-familiar-designs-why-websites-need-to-make-a-great-first-impression/
CENTRAL: T1; T2; T3; T4; T6; T7; T8
OPTIONAL: T5 (hero video or motion for consumer or brand-heavy launches)
SIT OUT: T1 (brand-anchored mode only): Inside an established design system the job is consistency, so seeds add brand drift. The brand system replaces the T2 vision, and canonical brand screens become the critic's references.
RISKS:
  - No conversion or trust A/B evidence yet that distinctive beats generic. The commercial case rests on credibility research and sentiment.
  - Divergence spent on structure hurts first impressions (prototypicality), so boldness should go into type, colour, imagery and one signature moment.
  - Critic quality is a risk: general vision models were near chance on UI quality (older models), so references, a taxonomy or a learned critic are needed.
  - The tell catalog goes stale as defaults migrate to second-order defaults.
  - Generated images bring their own tells (generic people, glossy sheen), so T7 must cover media too.
  - The cost and latency of parallel seeded runs plus critic loops inside builders.
SUCCESS:
  - 3-5 seeded directions that a blind panel rates as clearly different, not colour swaps.
  - Zero patterns on an automated tell scan of the final page (current catalog, e.g. Krebs-style checks).
  - The blind critic ranks the page at or above the median of professional category exemplars.
  - Fewer sections and elements than the first build, with real generated or product imagery instead of CSS blobs.
  - Headline and CTA rewritten by the founder, with a launch page shipped in about an hour.

### #4 Presentation decks and one-pagers: client, sales, investor, leadership and conference decks
PERSONA: Consultants and analysts (junior staff especially), sales and solutions engineers, founders preparing investor decks, managers preparing leadership updates, and educators, using Gamma, Copilot in PowerPoint, NotebookLM, Claude, Lilli or Deckster.
CORE_PAIN: The same prompt gives the same deck. Output shows template sameness, text overload and filler slides, repetitive or irrelevant images, and no storyline. Audiences read it as no effort, and decks are explicitly named as workslop artifacts.
EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: Moderate. Strong benchmarks exist on generation and critique (AutoPresent, CVPR 2025; SlideAudit, UIST 2025), along with a first-party admission of identical output (Microsoft) and large market data (Gamma). Evidence of pain at the audience level is mostly anecdotal or inferred from the workslop studies.
MARKET_REALITY: Usage is large: Gamma about $100M ARR and 70M users, Genspark $250M annualized, McKinsey Lilli used monthly by over 75% of staff. The standalone category is being absorbed into assistants and office suites, so the protocol should run as a layer over the host's output (PPTX, Canva, Gamma), not as another deck tool. Vendors already charge more for better image models (Gamma Ultra at $100/mo), which shows T4 has value.
FREQUENCY_AND_STAKES: Weekly for consultants, sales and managers. Per-instance stakes can be high (client pitches, board or investor decks, lost deals) and are reputational.
WHY_PROTOCOL_FITS: Decks combine the document pain (no thesis, padding) with the visual pain (template sameness, filler imagery). T2 sets the narrative spine, T6 cuts slide count and bullet density, T4 handles imagery, T7 covers deck tells and T8 rewrites action titles. An external, taxonomy-armed T3 is needed because self-refinement barely helps. Rank rationale: full block fit and frequent high-stakes use, but audience-level evidence is mostly anecdotal and the standalone market is being absorbed.
RENDERED_VIEW: Each slide rendered as an image at presentation resolution, plus a contact sheet of all slides and a titles-only read-through that tests whether the action titles alone tell the story. Shown among the organisation's best past decks or professional consulting decks. No outline, prompt or speaker rationale.
HUMAN_VOICE_SURFACES: Action titles, the one-sentence storyline or exec summary, the speaker notes or talk track, and the closing ask.
EVIDENCE:
  - All models fell short of human-designed slides, and self-refinement added only about 1-3 points over three rounds — AutoPresent, CVPR 2025 — https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00912v2
  - LLMs identified slide design flaws at F1 0.331-0.655. Prompting with an expert flaw taxonomy did best and improved remediation — SlideAudit, UIST 2025 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/41c87a09085f5bac9ce17d101ca05565/
  - Copilot 'might generate the same or very similar content for multiple people' — Microsoft Copilot in PowerPoint FAQ — https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/office/frequently-asked-questions-about-copilot-in-powerpoint-3e229188-9086-4f4c-9f9f-824cd25ae84f
  - Slides rated lower quality were more likely to be rated AI-generated — Leinonen et al. 2026 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/1c486065bc575d12afd90c490c457340/
  - A vendor rep presented an unread 30+ slide AI deck and lost the business — Hacker News (anecdotal) — https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48938128
  - Gamma passed $100M ARR with 70M users and over 1M creations a day — VCA Online (Nov 2025) — https://www.vcaonline.com/news/2025111004/gamma-surpasses-100m-arr-raises-at-2-1b-valuation-as-it-replaces-powerpoint-for-the-ai-era/
  - Standalone AI slides have 'collapsed into a feature' across Claude, ChatGPT, Canva and M365 — Sacra (May 2026) — https://sacra.com/research/rip-ai-slides-2022-2026/
CENTRAL: T2 (storyline spine first, drafted as action titles); T3 (taxonomy-armed, ranked against reference decks); T4; T6; T7; T8
OPTIONAL: T1 (two contrasting visual treatments or storylines for high-stakes investor or board decks)
SIT OUT: T5: Business decks are rarely video-led. Motion adds production risk with no evidence that it pays off.
RISKS:
  - Hosts differ in how they render and edit (Gamma, PowerPoint, Canva), and the critic needs consistent renders.
  - Visual polish can hide a weak argument (SLATE found artifact quality dissociated from effectiveness), so the critic must judge storyline, not just the look.
  - Hallucinated numbers in decks are outside the blocks.
  - As the category commoditises, value has to come from the quality layer.
SUCCESS:
  - A titles-only read-through tells the full story, with one message per slide.
  - Fewer slides and fewer words per slide than the AI first draft.
  - Zero deck tells on the catalog audit (emoji bullets, generic 'Key Takeaways', gradient title cards).
  - The blind critic ranks the deck in the top tier of the organisation's best decks.
  - No 'AI made this' remarks from the audience, and the decision is reached in the meeting.

### #5 Agent-written code changes: lean diffs and independent verification (including agent-written tests and PR text)
PERSONA: Senior and staff engineers and tech leads who delegate to Claude Code, Cursor or Codex; the reviewers and maintainers who inherit the diffs; and DevEx or platform teams designing agent pipelines and CI gates.
CORE_PAIN: Agents over-produce: unrequested abstractions, duplication, comments that narrate the code, defensive fallbacks and bigger PRs. Output is 'almost right'. Self-review is biased, so the bottleneck moves to human review, and delivery stability drops.
EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: Strong. Stack Overflow 2025 (49k+ respondents), the METR RCT, GitClear (211M changed lines), Faros telemetry (10k+ developers), and multiple TOSEM, ICLR and NeurIPS papers on agent PRs and self-correction. Some vendor studies (CodeRabbit, Sonar) come from companies with a commercial interest.
MARKET_REALITY: The largest spend: coding was $4.0B of $7.3B enterprise departmental AI in 2025 (Menlo). Cursor is reported at $4B annualized and Claude Code at $2.5B. The blocks are already partly productized here: Cursor's deslop skill, Anthropic's code-simplifier and /simplify, fresh-context /code-review, CodeRabbit, and Cursor's Graphite acquisition. An anti-over-engineering CLAUDE.md has 217.1k stars. The protocol's contribution is the wiring (blind, reference-ranked, hidden threshold, subtraction that guards against critic-induced bloat), not new capabilities.
FREQUENCY_AND_STAKES: Many times a day per engineer. Stakes are delivery stability (DORA: -7.2% for each 25% increase in AI adoption), the review bottleneck, maintainability debt and production bugs.
WHY_PROTOCOL_FITS: Over-production is the dominant failure (T6). A de facto tell catalog exists (T7). Self-review fails while independent, stronger-model feedback helps (T3). The divergence blocks correctly sit out for routine changes. Rank rationale: strongest evidence and largest spend, but only three blocks do the work, much of the pain is correctness, and incumbents already ship analogues of T3, T6 and T7.
RENDERED_VIEW: The unified diff plus observed behaviour (test and CI output, and screenshots for UI changes), together with the original acceptance criteria. Never the agent's transcript, plan or PR rationale. The reference set is exemplary merged PRs from the same repo, and mutation score serves as the bar for tests.
HUMAN_VOICE_SURFACES: The PR body's 'why / tradeoffs / how verified', issue reports, review replies, public API names and user-facing error messages. Routine commit messages can stay AI-written, since raters prefer LLM commit messages.
EVIDENCE:
  - 66% cite 'almost right, but not quite' as their top frustration, 45.2% say debugging AI code takes longer, and 3.1% highly trust AI accuracy — Stack Overflow 2025 — https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai
  - Copy/pasted lines rose from 8.3% to 12.3%, and refactor-associated (moved) lines fell from 25% to under 10% (2021-2024) — GitClear — https://www.gitclear.com/ai_assistant_code_quality_2025_research
  - On high-adoption teams, PR size rose 154%, review time 91% and bugs per developer 9% — Faros AI — https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-software-engineering
  - Average self-correction blind spot of 64.5% across 14 models — Self-Correction Bench — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02778
  - Self-repair is limited by feedback quality, and feedback from a stronger model gives much larger gains — Olausson et al., ICLR 2024 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09896
  - 45.1% of merged Claude Code PRs needed human revision, and the merge rate was 83.8% vs 91.0% for human PRs — Watanabe et al. — https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14745v3
  - Developers were 19% slower with AI while believing they were 20% faster — METR RCT (Jul 2025) — https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/
  - Reviewers told to find gaps always find some, and chasing every finding over-engineers. Separately, bot review raised PR closure time from 5h52m to 8h20m (Cihan et al., ICSE-SEIP 2025) — Claude Code best practices — https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices
CENTRAL: T3 (different model, sees only the diff and behaviour, hidden threshold, findings limited to correctness and requirements); T6 (scope-level: files, helpers, features, comments); T7 (deterministic deslop catalog run before the critic)
OPTIONAL: T8 (PR intent paragraph and user-facing strings); T2 (spec interview when requirements are vague)
SIT OUT: T1: Variance is unwanted in routine changes, which should be consistent with existing patterns. Route architecture choices to the Divergence Engine instead.; T4: No assets. Deterministic tools such as fuzzers and codemods are specialists, but this block is about media generators.; T5: No media pipeline.
RISKS:
  - The market is crowded with tools that sell T3, T6 and T7 analogues, so differentiation has to come from the blind, hidden-threshold wiring and from measured outcomes.
  - Critic noise and over-verification cost: bot comments lengthened PR cycle time, and vendors now discourage generic double-checks. Use the gate selectively and scope its findings.
  - Reward hacking: agents edit tests or special-case inputs (ImpossibleBench), so the implementer must not see or edit the gate.
  - T6 can over-compress logic into clever one-liners. It should target scope, not logic density.
  - Correctness and security defects need tests and security tooling outside the blocks.
SUCCESS:
  - Diff size and duplicated blocks drop against an unassisted-agent baseline at the same test pass rate.
  - Merge rate rises, and the share of merged agent PRs that need human revision falls below the 45.1% baseline.
  - Review time per PR falls with no rise in post-merge incidents.
  - Agent-written tests clear a hidden mutation-kill bar, with zero test-file edits by the implementer.
  - Reviewers report near-zero AI tells (narrating comments, single-use helpers, impossible-case fallbacks).

### #6 Ad creative and campaign assets: static, short-form video and creator-style content
PERSONA: Brand and performance marketers, creative directors, agencies, and SMB or DTC advertisers using Meta Advantage+ gen-AI tools, image and video models (Runway, Kling, Veo, HeyGen), plus creators producing sponsored content.
CORE_PAIN: Consumers spot AI ads and dislike them. Even high-quality AI ads leave weaker memory traces. Image generation collapses toward a few generic motifs at any temperature. Using the LLM as a ghostwriter anchors experts and lowers clicks. Advertisers badly overestimate how positive consumers feel.
EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: Moderate to strong. Strong peer-reviewed work: Hintze et al. (Patterns), Chen et al. (Management Science), Grigsby et al. (JRCS), Kirk et al. (JBR). Industry studies: NIQ EEG (n>2,000), IAB/Sonata, Billion Dollar Boy. Counter-evidence: Hartmann et al. (IJRM) found AI images earned +50% CTR versus stock. Video-ad-specific evidence is thin.
MARKET_REALITY: More than 8M advertisers had used a Meta AI creative tool by Q1 2026 (company-reported). AI video vendors' ARR estimates (Kling about $500M, Runway about $200M, HeyGen about $200M) are aggregator figures. Builders are adding metered image and video generation. Live A/B testing is the incumbent 'critic'. Agencies are likely buyers, but they were not researched.
FREQUENCY_AND_STAKES: Continuous for performance marketers fighting creative fatigue, with many variants a week. Brand campaigns are less frequent but carry public-backlash risk (e.g. Coca-Cola's AI holiday ads, anecdotal).
WHY_PROTOCOL_FITS: This is the only use case where specialist pipelines with state handoff (T4/T5) are the production medium. Convergence to a few motifs regardless of temperature needs external seeds (T1). 'Soulless' is a concept failure (T2). Visual tells drive identification (T7). Taglines and VO scripts are voice surfaces (T8). A blind pre-launch gate (T3) catches what producers misjudge. Rank rationale: full-stack fit and real backlash evidence, but critics judge creative work poorly and A/B testing already acts as a judge.
RENDERED_VIEW: The finished ad as served: a static image at feed-placement size, or video at true length with sound, inside a mobile-feed mock. Shown shuffled among best-in-category and award-winning ads, with no brief, concept rationale or prompt.
HUMAN_VOICE_SURFACES: Tagline, headline, hook line, VO script and captions. Use real human faces and voices where the message is emotional.
EVIDENCE:
  - Consumers identified most AI ads and rated them more annoying, boring and confusing, with weaker memory activation even when quality was high — NielsenIQ (2,000+, about 150 with EEG) — https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2024/niq-research-uncovers-hidden-consumer-attitudes-toward-ai-generated-ads/
  - 82% of ad executives think Gen Z and Millennials feel positive about AI ads, but only 45% do, and the gap is widening — IAB / Sonata (Jan 2026) — https://www.iab.com/?p=203870
  - Preference for gen-AI creator content fell from 60% to 26% while 79% of marketers increase spend on it — Billion Dollar Boy — https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-agency-news/new-billion-dollar-boy-research-global-ad-spend-surges-on-ai-creator-content-despite-growing-consumer-resistance-to-ai-slop/
  - 700 image-loop trajectories converged to 12 generic motifs at every temperature tested — Hintze et al., Patterns 2025 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12827715/
  - Using the LLM as a ghostwriter anchored experts and gave no click benefit. Using it as a sounding board lifted non-experts close to expert level — Chen et al., Management Science 2024 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/61348a7ae0cf5ad2920a275189cc4d31/?utm_source=claude_desktop
  - AI disclosure lowers trust, which recovers when AI makes only tangible elements and real people stay real — Grigsby et al., JRCS 2025 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/0655e95db60a5a0587e3624fd417df33/?utm_source=claude_desktop
  - Across 678 practising creatives, the best model beat the worst only about 61% of the time, and LLM judges correlated weakly with human rankings — Creativity Benchmark 2025 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/582cbfb819e65f998f4d42185c3bb4d2/?utm_source=claude_desktop
  - Counter-evidence: AI marketing images scored higher on quality and realism, and an AI banner got about 50% higher CTR than stock — Hartmann, Exner & Domdey, IJRM 2025 — https://www.mgt.tum.de/our-stories-with-impact/stories/the-power-of-generative-ai-in-visual-marketing
  - A practitioner pipeline chains image, voice and video models using anchor portraits, start frames and voice references for continuity — dawndrain/movie-gen — https://github.com/dawndrain/movie-gen
CENTRAL: T1; T2; T4; T5; T7 (visual and verbal, run per shot); T3 (pre-launch gate; live A/B is the outer judge); T8
OPTIONAL: T6 (one message, fewer elements; ads are already short)
SIT OUT: T5 (static-only placements): When a campaign is static images only, the video chain is not used.
RISKS:
  - LLM and VLM judges are weak predictors of creative quality, so the critic must be anchored to proven ads and recalibrated against live performance.
  - Synthetic human likeness raises trust and backlash risk, so keep real people for emotional content.
  - Disclosure labels themselves depress engagement, and the rules depend on platform policy.
  - Because AI images can beat stock, the bar is best-in-category work, not the average human.
  - Video continuity is technically fragile, and each iteration is expensive.
SUCCESS:
  - A creative-director panel judges the concepts distinct, and parallel variant sets show low pairwise similarity.
  - In pre-tests, consumers flag the ad as AI no more often than human-made controls.
  - CTR, recall and memory metrics at or above human-made baselines, with no public backlash.
  - Zero visual tells on the per-shot audit (warped logos or hands, motif cliches).
  - Taglines and VO scripts written by humans.

### #7 Option-space exploration: strategy options, product and campaign concepts, research ideas, architecture and stack choices
PERSONA: Strategists, consultants, PMs, founders, innovation and R&D teams, researchers generating hypotheses, and staff engineers and architects choosing stacks and designs.
CORE_PAIN: AI-assisted ideation narrows idea sets and makes everyone's options alike, both within one model and across models. 'Be creative' prompts, temperature and switching models don't fix this. LLM self-evaluation of ideas is unreliable. Users feel less ownership. Coding agents converge on a default stack however the request is phrased.
EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: Strong. This is the strongest academic base in the sweeps (Nature Human Behaviour, Science Advances, PNAS Nexus, a NeurIPS Best Paper, ICLR, and the BCG/HBS field experiment), plus moderate software-engineering evidence (Amplifying's 2,430 runs, PlanSearch).
MARKET_REALITY: There is no standalone paid category. Variation tools exist (Variant at $20/mo) and still cluster when starting from scratch. Figma reports that 60% of successful AI projects explored multiple approaches (correlational). The value shows up as the front-end of other workflows (campaign territories, content angles, architecture), and in consulting, where strategic sameness is a competitive risk. The pain is invisible to individuals because their own output improves, so demand has to be created at team or leadership level.
FREQUENCY_AND_STAKES: Episodic: kickoffs, planning cycles, campaign starts, greenfield architecture. But these decisions are upstream and high-leverage. A converged option set caps everything built after it, and competitors using the same model reach the same recommendation.
WHY_PROTOCOL_FITS: T1 is exactly the kind of structured, external intervention the evidence favours over temperature or persona prompts. T2's broad list, then human reaction, then sharpening mirrors the human-led mode that preserved diversity and ownership. T6 prunes duplicates. T7, run as a 'default idea' audit, flags anything a vanilla model also produces. T3 has to be a multi-model panel anchored to prior art. Rank rationale: strongest science and the best T1 fit, but the pain is invisible to individuals and there is no standalone market, so most of its value is delivered inside other configurations.
RENDERED_VIEW: The option set as plain, uniform cards (title plus a two-line description), shuffled and mixed with prior-art or competitor options and a vanilla-model baseline set, with no seed or generation metadata. For architecture, one-page sketches in a uniform template.
HUMAN_VOICE_SURFACES: The selection itself (human taste), the names and one-line pitches of shortlisted options, and the final rationale for the chosen option, such as an ADR 'why' in the architect's own words.
EVIDENCE:
  - ChatGPT-assisted idea sets were less diverse in 37 of 45 comparisons. In one task 94% of ideas overlapped, and nine participants independently named their toy 'Build-a-Breeze Castle' — Meincke, Nave & Terwiesch, Nature Human Behaviour 2025 — https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/2025/new-in-nature-chatgpt-decreases-idea-diversity-in-brainstorming/
  - 758 BCG consultants scored about 40% higher on quality with GPT-4, with 41% lower idea diversity — BCG — https://www.bcg.com/publications/2023/how-people-create-and-destroy-value-with-gen-ai
  - Both intra-model repetition and inter-model homogeneity across 26K open-ended queries — Artificial Hivemind, NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954
  - Only about 5% of 4,000 LLM research ideas were non-duplicates, and LLM judges agreed with experts less than experts agreed with each other — Si et al., ICLR 2025 — https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04109v1
  - 2,400 LLM strategy recommendations compressed strategic diversity toward conformity ('Synthetic Optimism') — Stoeber et al., JBR 2026 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/e006c172ecf051be82882db4db1e0631/
  - A model-led mode reduced diversity and ownership, while a human-led reflective mode improved quality and kept both (N=486, replicated with N=640) — Maier et al., CHI 2026 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/3458ef80925e513ca75d63fdc8e4ecef/
  - Typicality bias in preference data drives mode collapse, and verbalized sampling raised diversity 1.6-2.1x — Zhang et al. 2025 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171
  - Claude Code returned the same stack about 76% of the time across rephrasings (2,430 runs) — Amplifying (Feb 2026) — https://amplifying.ai/research/how-ai-coding-agents-choose-your-stack
  - Search gains were predicted by the diversity of generated plans: pass@200 of 77.0% vs 60.6% for repeated sampling — PlanSearch, ICLR 2025 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03733
CENTRAL: T1; T2; T6 (merge near-duplicates, cut the pool)
OPTIONAL: T3 (multi-model panel ranking against prior art; the human makes the final call); T7 (conceptual 'default idea' audit against a vanilla baseline); T4 (mood boards for shortlisted concepts); T8 (naming and pitch lines for the chosen option)
SIT OUT: T5: No media at the ideation stage.
RISKS:
  - Individual users don't see the pain because their own output improves, so adoption depends on team or organisational framing.
  - LLM judges are unreliable on ideas, and a single critic will pull the shortlist back to the mode.
  - Random seeds can produce off-brief noise unless they are interpreted against constraints.
  - No study tests random seed strings directly. The closest evidence is random-word infusion (arXiv 2601.18053, cited in the practitioner sweep), Oblique Strategies cards and verbalized sampling.
  - For architecture the goal is fit, not novelty, so seeds should vary constraints and reference systems, not fashion.
SUCCESS:
  - The option set's semantic spread matches or beats an unaided human group, with less overlap with a vanilla baseline.
  - Parallel runs and different team members produce different shortlists.
  - Human-rated quality of the top options at or above unaided AI.
  - The team reports owning the chosen direction.
  - For architecture: at least three materially different options, from different stack families, before committing.

### #8 Platform-level default quality for AI app builders, coding agents and skill authors (headless, at scale)
PERSONA: Product and AI engineers at Lovable, v0, Bolt, Replit, Figma Make and coding-agent vendors, plus authors of design and writing skills and plugins.
CORE_PAIN: Millions of users send similar thin prompts to the same models, so a platform's output becomes a recognisable look that devalues users' products. A single generation cannot see its siblings, so a per-run instruction to 'be different' can't be followed. Bans spawn second-order defaults, and generic vision models can't gate quality.
EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: Moderate. First-party statements (Anthropic blog and SKILL.md), a single-author controlled comparison (Wetch), UIClip (strong), Shin et al. (conceptual), and adoption signals.
MARKET_REALITY: Platforms hold distribution and control model routing. They are already adding design systems (Lovable, Aug-Sep 2026) and image and video generators (Veo 3.1, GPT Image), and shipping skills. This is probably the channel through which most users would meet the protocol, which fits the finding that standalone generators get commoditized.
FREQUENCY_AND_STAKES: Every generation, at platform scale. Stakes are platform reputation and users' products being dismissed as 'vibe-coded', plus the ecosystem loop in which homogenized output becomes training data.
WHY_PROTOCOL_FITS: Only the platform can enforce diversity across generations, by putting T1 outside the model. It can also run always-on T7 detectors and a T3 ranker over best-of-N candidates, and T4 routing is a platform decision. Rank rationale: this is the distribution channel and the only actor that can enforce cross-generation diversity, but the evidence is first-party or conceptual and no human taste is in the loop.
RENDERED_VIEW: Screenshots of N candidate outputs for one project, plus a random sample of recent sibling outputs for similar prompts from other projects, so the critic can judge cross-project sameness. No prompts, code or seeds shown.
HUMAN_VOICE_SURFACES: The platform owns none. It flags placeholder copy (headline, CTA, about) and prompts the user to rewrite it, and it offers direction menus at onboarding.
EVIDENCE:
  - The instruction 'NEVER converge across generations' can't be followed because each run is isolated. A rewritten skill won 21 of 28 decisive comparisons (p=0.0125) — Justin Wetch (Jan 2026) — https://www.justinwetch.com/blog/improvingclaudefrontend
  - The official skill now has to list second-order defaults — anthropics frontend-design SKILL.md — https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/claude-code/main/plugins/frontend-design/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md
  - Generic vision models judged UI quality near chance, while a specialised critic reached 75.1% — UIClip, UIST 2024 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12500
  - Homogenization concentrates at the prompt and refine stages because of 'frictionless' interaction design. The authors propose style subfloors and 'productive friction' — Shin et al. 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13036
  - Impeccable ships 60 deterministic AI-slop detector rules and has 77.1k stars — https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable
  - Design Arena drew 47k+ users in 4 weeks for a taste benchmark — YC Launch — https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/O5h-design-arena-1-benchmark-for-ai-design
  - Lovable is nearing 8M users and 100k products built per day — TechCrunch (Nov 2025) — https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/10/lovable-says-its-nearing-8-million-users-as-the-year-old-ai-coding-startup-eyes-more-corporate-employees
CENTRAL: T1 (seed per project or session, enforced outside the model); T7 (always-on deterministic detectors); T3 (blind learned ranker over best-of-N); T4 (asset routing)
OPTIONAL: T2 (onboarding menu of contrasting style subfloors); T6 (default trim); T5 (premium or brand tiers)
SIT OUT: T8: A platform cannot supply the user's voice. T8 is reduced to flagging placeholder copy for the user to rewrite.
RISKS:
  - With no human taste per artifact, the ranker must be learned and calibrated on human preference data, or it re-centres on the mode.
  - Seeds that break structural conventions hurt first impressions.
  - The compute cost of best-of-N plus ranking at platform scale.
  - Diversity metrics can be gamed with superficial palette swaps.
SUCCESS:
  - Cross-project similarity for similar prompts drops measurably, so outputs no longer cluster into one look.
  - Default tells on automated scans are near zero across a sample of projects.
  - The ranker agrees with human preference data (Design Arena style) above a set bar.
  - Placeholder copy is flagged in every output, and the user rewrite rate is tracked.

### #9 Signal-bearing and personal-voice documents: cover letters, freelance proposals, pitches, grant and RFP responses, application essays
PERSONA: Senders: job seekers, freelancers, student applicants, founders pitching and bid teams. Receivers: recruiters, hiring managers, investors, admissions readers and procurement reviewers.
CORE_PAIN: One-click AI tailoring destroyed the signal value of writing. Documents are formulaic even when personal details are supplied, so receivers discount them and shift to other signals. Senders need a credible way to show real fit and effort.
EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: Strong. Field data (Freelancer.com before and after its tool launched; a large labor-platform difference-in-differences study), Management Science experiments, a comparison of 30,000 essays, and evidence of LLM screener self-preference.
MARKET_REALITY: Mostly individual consumers. Platforms such as Freelancer.com embedded AI proposal tools, and receivers are moving to work samples and work history. There is no clear paid product on the quality side, and the large humanizer market here is mostly evasion, which the protocol must not serve. Non-generative grammar help raised hires 8% (Wiles 2023), so polishing for clarity is legitimate.
FREQUENCY_AND_STAKES: Episodic for any one person but very high stakes per instance (a job, contract, admission or funding), and very high volume in aggregate.
WHY_PROTOCOL_FITS: The evidence points to human effort and sender-specific facts as the fix: editing time predicts success, and the penalty shrinks when AI only edits. The inverted, human-led configuration uses T6, T7 and T3 as auditors on a human draft, and T2 to surface angles from the person's own material. It cannot restore the signal at market level, but it helps an honest sender's specifics come through. Rank rationale: strong evidence and high per-instance stakes, but the remedy is mostly that the human writes it, the market-level signal can't be restored, and the risk of misuse is high.
RENDERED_VIEW: The document as the receiver sees it (a PDF or plain text in an applicant system), anonymized and placed in a pile of N real strong and average submissions for the same opportunity, with no information about how it was produced.
HUMAN_VOICE_SURFACES: All of it. AI text is a placeholder and the human writes every sentence. AI may only suggest angles, flag cuts and tells, and rank.
EVIDENCE:
  - After one-click AI proposals launched, employers stopped paying a premium for customized applications. The structural model estimates the top-ability quintile is hired 19% less often — Galdin & Silbert, via Tuck — https://tuck.dartmouth.edu/news/articles/what-happens-to-hiring-when-ai-writes-job-applications
  - An AI cover-letter tool raised callbacks, but the correlation between alignment and callbacks fell 51%. Time spent editing AI drafts correlated with hiring success — Cui et al. 2025 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/2a8b3c9f43125b5b85fef9aa83c24bee/
  - Senders' access to ChatGPT lowered screening accuracy by 4-9% in hiring and start-up investing — Cowgill et al., Management Science 2026 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/165d47248430557aaae499a43d12e7f3/
  - Compared with 30,000 human essays, LLM essays listed personal details formulaically even with persona prompts, and a classifier separated them almost perfectly — Cornell (COLM 2025) — https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2025/09/ai-can-write-your-college-essay-it-wont-sound-you
  - LLM screeners preferred their own generated resumes 68-88% of the time — Xu et al. 2025 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/b1ae19e8195f58f88851ad24b1df8be0/
CENTRAL: T8 (the human writes the whole document); T7 (flags formula and tells); T6 (marks cuts; does not rewrite)
OPTIONAL: T2 (angles mined from the person's own CV, notes and stories); T3 (ranks the draft against real exemplars; must be a different model from any screener)
SIT OUT: T1: Distinctiveness must come from true facts about the sender, not injected randomness.; T4: Not visual. Portfolios and pitch decks go through Studio Build.; T5: Not applicable.
RISKS:
  - Misuse for detector evasion or misrepresentation (academic integrity, fabricated specifics). The protocol must never invent facts and must be positioned as a quality tool.
  - It can't fix the collapse of signal at market level, and any benefit to one applicant may come at another's expense.
  - AI screeners prefer AI-written text, so a human voice may be penalised in some machine-screened pipelines.
  - Critic exemplars of 'strong essays' could teach a new formula.
SUCCESS:
  - The document contains verifiable, sender-specific detail and no listed-credential formula.
  - Zero catalog tells, and the blind critic ranks it in the top quartile of a real pile.
  - Every sentence is written or rewritten by the human, and the author can explain every claim.
  - Callback or acceptance rates are tracked against the sender's prior baseline.

### #10 Teaching and training content: lesson plans, worksheets, course materials, L&D modules
PERSONA: K-12 teachers (60% used AI in 2024-25), faculty, instructional designers and corporate L&D teams.
CORE_PAIN: AI lesson plans are formulaic and follow one template whatever the topic. They skew toward low-level 'remember' tasks, lack cultural and local context, and drift away from the pedagogy requested. LLM graders miss deep pedagogical quality.
EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: Moderate. One strong content analysis (CITE, 310 plans), several smaller domain studies and a large Gallup survey. The sweeps contain no evidence for T5 in training video.
MARKET_REALITY: Adoption among teachers is wide, but individual willingness to pay is low. Districts and edtech platforms are the buyers. AI training video exists in corporate L&D, but the sweeps found no quality evidence for it.
FREQUENCY_AND_STAKES: Weekly preparation. Stakes accumulate as learning quality across a term, with little visibility in any single instance.
WHY_PROTOCOL_FITS: T2's vivid pedagogical vision directly counters drift and low-level defaults. T1 can vary activity formats across a unit. T6 cuts template sections. T8 adds the local classroom detail that experts reward. T3 can check rubric-visible features but needs a human expert for depth. Rank rationale: formulaic output is real and measured, and adoption is wide, but willingness to pay is low and the rendered artifact is a weak proxy for learning outcomes.
RENDERED_VIEW: The student-facing materials (worksheet, slides, activity instructions) as printed or projected, plus the teacher-facing plan, shown among strong human-made plans for the same standard, with no generation prompt.
HUMAN_VOICE_SURFACES: Directions to students, discussion prompts, local or class-specific context and examples, and the teacher's framing of the lesson's purpose.
EVIDENCE:
  - Across 2,230 activities, 45% were 'remember'-level and about 10% higher-order. 94% of plans lacked multicultural content, and all three chatbots used the same template — Trust & Maloy, CITE Journal 2025 — https://citejournal.org/volume-25/issue-3-25/social-studies/civic-education-in-the-age-of-ai-should-we-trust-ai-generated-lesson-plans/
  - 22 of 60 inquiry-based plans scored under 50% of the maximum and drifted toward content knowledge — Grossmann et al. 2025 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/4db08d4cba205e4e9aabdfb1aed9892c/
  - Teachers rated human plans higher (d=0.44), judging on specific detail and classroom knowledge — Cooper 2024 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/6a127358f4e954dba2b3b1776f1683a7/
  - An LLM grader agreed moderately with experts on explicit features but was weak on depth of pedagogical reasoning — Hauk et al. 2025 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/b6f9a48e2a9f599caf232a23f011ff8f/
  - 60% of teachers used AI, and weekly users save 5.9 hours a week — Gallup / Walton (n=2,232) — https://news.gallup.com/poll/691967/three-teachers-weekly-saving-six-weeks-year.aspx
  - Artifact quality is dissociated from instructional effectiveness — SLATE 2026 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/a5517c86a3e25a67827bfe4b6e45378c/
CENTRAL: T2 (pedagogical vision: inquiry-led, higher-order, culturally grounded); T6; T8
OPTIONAL: T1 (varying activity formats across a unit); T3 (rubric and exemplar critic with human expert sign-off); T4 (diagrams and illustrations)
SIT OUT: T7: Learners care about the cognitive demand of the tasks, not prose tells, so a tell audit adds little.; T5: The sweeps hold no quality evidence for AI training video.
RISKS:
  - The blind critic sees the artifact, but quality shows up in learning outcomes (the SLATE dissociation), so the loop may optimise the wrong thing.
  - LLM critics miss high-inference pedagogy, and expert time is scarce.
  - Seeds could introduce inaccurate or culturally inappropriate content.
SUCCESS:
  - The share of higher-order activities (analyse, evaluate, create) rises above the AI baseline, where 45% of activities were remember-level.
  - Plans include diverse perspectives and local context.
  - An expert teacher rates the plan on par with strong human-made plans.
  - The prep-time savings are kept.

## RECOMMENDED CONFIGS
- Studio Build (the article's design workflow) | covers: AI-built interfaces: landing pages, sites, MVP and product UI, prototypes (rank 3); Presentation decks and one-pagers, in deck mode (rank 4); Brand-anchored in-company UI, in brand mode (merged into rank 3)
  topology: Discover: fan out 3-5 parallel runs. Each run gets a different external seed string from a shell script (not from the model) that it must interpret into a direction, and returns low-detail direction cards, broad not deep (T1 crossed with the first step of T2). Define: the human reacts to favourites, the AI sharpens the chosen direction to the human's taste and writes the build brief. The vision is often borrowed from another domain, and boldness goes into brand surfaces (type, colour, imagery, one signature moment) while structure stays conventional (T2). Deliver: the implementer builds. Every asset request goes through a nested specialist sub-pipeline: T4 image models for hero, illustration and texture, and T5 only for hero video or motion, using keyframe chaining or generate-in-context-then-matte. Refine: render, run a cheap deterministic T7 tell scan, then send screenshots to the T3 blind critic. The critic is a different or stronger model in a fresh context. It sees only desktop and mobile screenshots shuffled among 5-10 professional references, carries a flaw taxonomy, and returns a rank and a score. The implementer iterates until a threshold the critic never sees. Finish: T6 subtractive pass ('what does this page's job require?'), then a full T7 catalog audit (visual, media and copy tells, versioned), then T8, where the human rewrites headline, subhead, CTAs and benefit lines in one voice. A final blind T3 check confirms the cuts and human copy did not cause a regression. Deck mode: T2 drafts the storyline as action titles first, T5 is off, and the critic uses a slide-flaw taxonomy and ranks against the organisation's best decks. Brand mode: T1 sits out, the brand system replaces the T2 vision, canonical brand screens become the critic's references, and T7 adds token-drift checks.
  distinct: The only configuration in which all eight blocks can run. It combines a parallel divergence front-end, a nested specialist asset sub-pipeline, and a critic loop that judges rendered pixels. Its unit of judgment is a rendered visual artifact.
- Divergence Engine (wide-then-narrow exploration) | covers: Option-space exploration: strategy, concepts, research ideas, architecture and stack (rank 7); Front-end module nested inside Seeded Angle to Owned Voice (ranks 1 and 10), Media Pipeline (rank 6) and Studio Build's Discover phase (rank 3); Architecture alternatives that the Lean Diff Gate hands off (rank 5)
  topology: Maximum parallelism. It ends in a decision, not an artifact. Fan out 8-20 runs (T1), each with its own external seed: a random string, a random concept or a random reference domain. Each run returns many low-detail options. Pool everything. T6 merges near-duplicates by embedding similarity and cuts the pool. T7 runs as a 'default idea' audit: a vanilla run of the same prompt with no seed, plus a catalog of known convergences (for example, the default stack), marks any option the baseline also produced. T3 is a panel, not a loop: two or three different-model critics see only shuffled option cards mixed with prior art and the vanilla baseline, rank them, and the ranks are aggregated. Then the human taste step (T2): the human stars and annotates favourites, and the AI sharpens two or three into briefs. The output is an option map plus a brief that another configuration consumes. T4 is optional (mood boards for the shortlist). T5 sits out, and T8 is used only to name the chosen option.
  distinct: T1 dominates and the work runs in parallel. The critic ranks many options rather than refining one artifact, and the configuration is a reusable front-end that other configurations nest, so the protocol's divergence half is defined in one place.
- Reader-Ready Document (subtract, audit, own) | covers: Internal workplace documents / workslop (rank 2); Text-first one-pagers and exec summaries (rank 4, light); PR descriptions, issues and agent context files (rank 5 text surfaces); Outreach email templates (rejected as a standalone use case, served here)
  topology: Strictly sequential and single-threaded, built to finish in minutes inside a copilot. Step 0 (light T2): the human writes one or two sentences giving the decision or ask and the one point the document must carry. The AI may offer five framings to react to. Then the AI drafts. T6 cuts to a length budget (one page, or under 100 words for an email) by asking what the reader would miss. T7 audits prose and structure tells: recap sections, reflexive triads, bold-bullet format bias. T3 is a blind 'recipient' critic: a fresh context and a different model that gets only the rendered document and the reader's role. It answers 'can I act on this in two minutes, what is missing, what is padding' and ranks the document against 3-5 exemplary internal documents. Findings are limited to substance and actionability, with at most 2-3 loops against a hidden threshold. T8: the human rewrites the bottom line, the ask, any judgment call and the sign-off. T1, T4 and T5 sit out. T1 can be nested from the Divergence Engine only when the document proposes options.
  distinct: The smallest and cheapest wiring: text only, with no divergence. The critic simulates a specific recipient rather than judging taste. It is built for the case where the sender doesn't feel the pain, so it has to be close to zero friction.
- Seeded Angle to Owned Voice (nested composition) | covers: Bylined and brand written content, including social and ghostwritten posts (rank 1); Teaching and training content, in pedagogical variant (rank 10)
  topology: A light Divergence Engine runs first: 5-8 seeds produce angle cards, which are flagged against a vanilla baseline. The human then picks the thesis from angles grounded in their own experience, data or classroom (T2). The AI drafts, or the human drafts for relational pieces. Then the polish spine: T6 cut, then T7 audit (a versioned prose catalog plus platform-specific tells such as formulaic hooks and emoji bullets), then a T3 blind critic that ranks the rendered piece among the author's own best work and category leaders against a hidden threshold. The author's corpus anchors style, and category leaders set the quality bar. Last, a heavy T8: the human rewrites headline, hook, transitions, kicker and CTA. An optional T4 branch runs in parallel to make header images, carousels or diagrams. Across a series (a content calendar or a teaching unit), seeds rotate and a portfolio-level similarity check runs over the last N pieces. In the teaching variant, the T2 vision is pedagogical (inquiry-led, higher-order, local context), T3 uses a rubric plus exemplar plans with a human expert sign-off, and T7 is light. T5 sits out.
  distinct: The only text configuration that puts divergence first and treats voice as the product. Its critic is anchored to the author's own corpus, and it is the only configuration that checks diversity across a series rather than within one artifact.
- Lean Diff Gate (critic-loop-centric verification) | covers: Agent-written code changes, tests and PR text (rank 5); Visual QA of agent-built frontends (merged); Agent context files and READMEs (merged)
  topology: Convergence only. The implementer works from a spec, with an optional T2-style interview when requirements are vague. The gate loop runs in this order. First, a deterministic T7 scan: linters plus a deslop catalog covering narrating comments, try/catch on trusted paths, casts to any, single-use helpers and impossible-case fallbacks. Second, a T6 cut at the level of scope: unrequested files, helpers, features and comments. It never compresses logic into clever one-liners. Third, a T3 blind reviewer: a different and often stronger model in a fresh context. It sees only the diff, the test and CI output, screenshots for UI changes, and the acceptance criteria, never the agent's transcript or PR rationale. It ranks the change against exemplary merged PRs from the same repo and reports only correctness and requirement findings. The implementer iterates until a hidden threshold is met, and a final T6 pass removes anything the critic loop added. Test sub-loop: a separate agent writes tests from the spec alone, the implementer cannot edit them, and mutation score is the objective bar. Exit: the human writes the PR's 'why / tradeoffs / how verified' paragraph (narrow T8). Run the gate selectively, on unattended runs and high-risk areas. T1, T4 and T5 sit out, and architecture choices go to the Divergence Engine.
  distinct: The critic gates a single artifact using objective signals (tests, mutation score) as well as references. Subtraction runs on both sides of the critic loop to stop review-induced bloat. There is no divergence and no media.
- Media Pipeline (specialist chain with state handoff) | covers: Ad creative and campaign assets, image and video (rank 6); Hero imagery and video for landing pages, nested inside Studio Build (rank 3); Brand social video, an optional branch of Seeded Angle to Owned Voice (rank 1)
  topology: Front: the Divergence Engine produces concept territories, and a creative director reacts and sharpens one into a brief and shot list (T1 and T2). Middle: a T4/T5 chain with explicit state handoff. An image model produces anchor portraits, product plates and style frames. Per-scene start frames condition a video model, and the last frame of clip n seeds clip n+1 (keyframe chaining). Products or characters are generated inside a real scene and extracted by matting. A voice model renders the VO from a human-written script. Real people are kept for faces wherever the message is emotional. Each shot passes a per-asset T7 visual tell audit (warped logos and hands, plastic sheen, frozen motion, stock motifs), and only failing shots are regenerated. T6 trims to one message and fewer elements. As a pre-launch gate, a T3 blind critic ranks finished ads at their served size and length against best-in-category ads. After launch, live A/B results act as the outer critic, and the hidden threshold is recalibrated against performance. T8: the human writes the tagline, headline, hook and VO script.
  distinct: The only configuration in which specialist generators are the production medium, state passes between models, audits run per shot, and an external market signal closes the loop over time.
- Human-Led Sounding Board (inverted authorship) | covers: Signal-bearing and personal-voice documents (rank 9); Fiction, memoir and scholarly clarity edits, when the author opts in (rejected as standalone targets); Relational or emotional posts from rank 1, at the author's choice
  topology: The human writes the draft, and AI text never ships. Optionally, before drafting, T2 lists many candidate angles mined from the person's own material (CV, notes, stories), broad not deep, and the human reacts. After drafting, the AI blocks run in parallel as auditors and return annotations, not rewrites. T6 marks cut candidates. T7 flags tells and formula: listed credentials, keywords echoed from the prompt, tidy self-announcing structure. The T3 blind critic ranks the draft inside a pile of N anonymized real exemplars of the genre and returns the rank plus the three most important weaknesses. The human revises, and the cycle repeats until the human stops (the threshold is optional). T1, T4 and T5 sit out.
  distinct: It inverts the pipeline: the human is the implementer and the blocks are critics. This matches the evidence that using AI as a sounding board beats ghostwriting, and that the human's editing effort is what carries the signal.
- Headless Platform Guardrail (automated best-of-N) | covers: Platform-level default quality for app builders, coding agents and skill authors (rank 8); Background default for rank 3 users who skip the full Studio Build
  topology: There is no human taste step per artifact. A seed is assigned per project or session outside the model (a random string at project creation), so sibling generations diverge by construction (T1). An optional T2 at onboarding offers a menu of contrasting 'style subfloors'. Generate N candidates in parallel. Always-on deterministic T7 detectors run against a versioned catalog, followed by a default T6 trim. A T3 blind ranker then picks the best candidate: a separate model, ideally a learned UI-quality model calibrated on human preference data. A cross-project similarity check compares the pick with recent sibling outputs for similar prompts. T4 routes imagery to specialist generators, so there are no CSS blobs. T8 is reduced to flagging placeholder copy for the user to rewrite. T5 runs only on premium or brand tiers. The catalog and ranker are recalibrated offline.
  distinct: It selects from N candidates rather than refining one, and it enforces diversity across many users' projects rather than within one session. With no per-artifact human, it is the only configuration that runs headless at platform scale.
## REJECTED
- AI deep-research and analyst reports / evidence briefs: The dominant pain is accuracy. Deep-research modes are one-sided, many of their statements are unsupported by their own sources, and citation accuracy is only 40-80% (DeepTRACE). Fabricated sources also appeared in Deloitte's government report. A blind critic, by design, cannot check claims against sources. Adding T6 and T8 polish to an unverified report makes its errors more persuasive. Revisit once a ninth, non-blind claim-to-source verification block exists.
- SEO and programmatic content at scale: Google's scaled-content-abuse policy targets content mass-produced to manipulate rankings, whether made by automation, humans or both. A protocol that makes templated pages read as less templated is therefore close to an evasion tool. A human voice pass on every page is economically impossible, and the real lever (original information and first-hand experience) is outside the blocks. The useful pattern, per-item seeding plus ranking against live competitors, is kept inside the Headless Platform Guardrail.
- Cold sales and outreach email as a standalone target: Relevance and data grounding are the biggest levers (71% of decision-makers cite irrelevance, per Hunter), along with deliverability. SDR-Bench shows models cannot predict which outreach lands. Both levers are outside the eight blocks. Templates can still run through Reader-Ready Document (T6 to under 100 words, T7 outreach tells, a T8 opening line).
- Scholarly papers and peer review: Venue integrity and disclosure policies make a tell audit easy to read as detection evasion. The headline harms are hallucinated citations (an accuracy problem) and reviewer overload, not sameness. Clarity-only edits on a human draft can use the Human-Led Sounding Board.
- Fiction and creative prose as a standalone target: The evidence is strong, but the paying market is small and hostile to AI provenance (the Authors Guild 'Human Authored' mark), and fine-tuning on other authors' books raises copyright questions. The pattern the evidence supports, human authorship with AI as critic, is already the Human-Led Sounding Board.
- Brand-anchored in-company UI, rich media for landing pages, and visual QA of agent-built frontends as separate use cases: These are merged rather than rejected on merit. Brand-anchored UI is a mode of Studio Build with T1 off, which suits a consistency job that design-system ingestion already partly serves. Rich media is Studio Build's nested T4/T5 sub-pipeline, or the Media Pipeline. Frontend visual QA is the Lean Diff Gate with screenshots as observed behaviour.
- AI-written tests, and repo docs or agent context files, as separate use cases: Merged. Tests become the Lean Diff Gate's test sub-loop. Evidence is moderate, and the reference bar (mutation score) comes from specialist tools rather than a block. Context files are essentially a T6-only job: vendor guidance already prescribes 'would removing this line cause a mistake?', and evidence on their value is mixed.
- Humanizing text to pass AI detectors (academic integrity, disclosure avoidance): This is misuse. Much humanizer demand is detector evasion, and helping it would undercut the trust the protocol is meant to earn. T7 and T8 must be positioned as quality and ownership steps that leave required disclosures intact.
- Correctness-first and security-first jobs (factual accuracy, hallucinations, code security, vibe-coded app security): These are real and severe pains, but they are correctness and verification problems. T3 helps only partly, and fixing them needs tests, retrieval, fact-checking and security tooling the protocol doesn't have. Every configuration should state that it does not guarantee correctness.
- Organizational causes of workslop and mandated 'AI productivity theater': Culture and incentive levers (team trust is associated with 61% less workslop; leadership communication matters), not output-pipeline blocks. They belong in adoption guidance around the Reader-Ready Document configuration.
## CROSS-CUTTING
- AI raises individual quality while shrinking collective diversity (BCG: about 40% higher quality with 41% lower diversity; Doshi & Hauser: more creative but up to 10.7% more similar stories; Zhou & Lee: more favourites, less novelty). Quality loops (T3) and divergence (T1/T2) act on different axes, so any configuration aimed at distinctiveness needs both. The diversity loss is also invisible to the individual, whose own output improves. That makes T1 most valuable where siblings are visible: teams, content series and platforms.
- Sameness holds across models and does not respond to temperature (Artificial Hivemind; Wenger & Kenett; Hintze's 12 motifs at every temperature). Switching or ensembling models is not a diversity strategy, and randomness has to come from outside the model. This supports T1, but no study tests the article's seed-string method directly. The closest evidence is random-word infusion, Oblique Strategies cards, verbalized sampling and chain-of-thought idea prompting.
- Bans move the mode rather than removing it. Anthropic's own skill now lists second-order defaults, and scanners already count the replacements as tells. T7 catalogs must therefore be versioned, applied after generation and paired with external seeds. No controlled study compares upfront bans with post-hoc audits, so the protocol should treat 'audit after' as a hypothesis and test it.
- Evidence-based design rules for T3, consistent across domains: (1) use a different, ideally stronger, model in a fresh context; (2) show only the rendered output, since stated rationale misses about 25% of the time (Design Theater); (3) rank against concrete references rather than giving an absolute score, because unanchored LLM critics are near-random on prose (WQ), slop (kappa about 0), UI (GPT-4V 51.6%) and creative work (Creativity Benchmark); (4) arm the critic with a flaw taxonomy (SlideAudit; UICrit +55%); (5) aggregate several judges for subjective work; (6) hide the threshold and scope findings to requirements, because gap-hunting critics always find something and cause over-engineering. The reference lineup for each use case is the critical asset to build.
- Over-production is the most universal failure: length-biased RLHF, addition bias across models, GitClear's duplication rise, LinkedIn post length up 107%, workslop padding, and 18% of expert edits being pure deletions. T6 is the most broadly applicable block. It should also run after any critic loop, because critics add material.
- The audience penalty attaches to perceived low effort or inauthenticity, not to AI use as such (Raptive: human text believed to be AI also loses trust). It is concentrated in emotional, relational and signal-bearing content and is minimal in transactional exchanges (Purcell). T8 is therefore essential for bylined, relational and signal-bearing work, optional for transactional text, and contra-indicated for routine summaries such as commit messages, where raters prefer LLM text.
- Who drafts first matters. AI-first ghostwriting anchors experts and lowered real ad clicks (Chen et al.). Model-led modes cut diversity and ownership, while human-led modes preserve both (Maier et al.). Configurations should place the human taste step (T2) before generation, and the protocol needs an inverted mode where the human drafts and the blocks critique.
- Spend divergence on brand surfaces, not structure. Prototypical, simple layouts win first impressions (Tuch et al.), and Anthropic's skill says to 'spend your boldness in one place'. Seeds and visions should vary type, colour, imagery, voice and one signature moment, while information architecture stays conventional.
- Accuracy sits outside the eight blocks. A blind critic cannot check claims against sources, and polishing unverified content makes it more persuasive (Deloitte). Research and analysis configurations need a ninth, non-blind verification block, and every configuration should state that it does not guarantee correctness.
- The market gap is clear. Incumbents productize grounding and consistency (design-system ingestion, brand kits, brand voice, content guardians). The protocol's distinctive blocks (T1, blind T3 with a hidden threshold, T6, post-hoc T7) mostly live in open-source skills with very large followings (Impeccable 77.1k, humanizer 54.2k, a Karpathy-style CLAUDE.md 217.1k). Standalone generators get commoditized (Jasper, AI slides), so configurations should ship as portable layers (skills, MCP) inside existing hosts. Platforms are the distribution channel.
- Who feels the pain decides adoption. In marketing, design and ads the creator's own audience punishes them, so users seek fixes. With workslop the cost lands on recipients and 55% admit sending it, so the configuration must be near-frictionless or built into copilots and org norms. Adoption design matters as much as block design.
- Speed gains outrun quality gains in every survey: Figma 78% more efficient vs 32% able to rely on output; CMI 87% more productive vs 39% better performance; Stack Overflow trust at 29%. Unlabelled AI often matches the average human. The unmet need is therefore a bar at best-in-category professional work plus portfolio-level diversity, not 'better than average'. Success metrics should be set at that bar.
- T7 and T8 are easy to misread as ways to disguise AI use (the disclosure paradox; a humanizer market driven by evasion). The protocol should frame them as quality and ownership steps, leave required disclosures intact, and never fabricate sender-specific facts.
## GAPS
- No controlled study compares upfront ban lists with post-hoc tell audits in any domain. The article's claim that bans make output stranger remains practitioner wisdom backed only by the 'second-order defaults' observation.
- No study tests the specific T1 mechanism of an external random seed string that the model must interpret. The closest evidence is random-word infusion (arXiv 2601.18053, cited only in the practitioner sweep), Oblique Strategies cards, verbalized sampling and chain-of-thought idea prompting.
- No A/B, conversion or trust experiment shows that a distinctive (non-AI-looking) page or deck outperforms a generic AI one. The commercial case for the article's own use case rests on Fogg 2002, first-impression research, adjacent ad studies and sentiment. McKinsey's design-value figures could not be verified.
- The hidden-threshold critic design is untested, with no evidence on optimal loop counts, threshold setting or whether hiding the threshold reduces grade inflation.
- Evidence on critic reliability for taste uses older models (GPT-4V in UIClip, GPT-4 in several writing studies). How reliable current frontier critics are on UI, prose and creative ranking is unknown.
- T5 evidence is thin outside ads: nothing on hero video for landing pages or on AI training video, and the sweeps did not gather video-ad-specific outcome data.
- T8's premise that human versions are shorter and plainer is only indirectly supported (expert-edit deletions, idiosyncratic voice cues). The time cost and outcome lift of a human voice pass have not been measured.
- The source article (Chimala, Lenny's Newsletter, Sep 2026) was not verified by most sweeps, and one could not find it at all. Technique definitions come from the brief.
- Several market figures are unverified, or are vendor or aggregator estimates: Lovable at about $600M, LinkedIn's 40% view cut for slop-classified posts, Workday figures via trade press, AI video ARRs, humanizer traffic, Gartner's 'acoustic brands' prediction, and Reddit-sourced over-engineering numbers.
- Prevalence statistics based on detectors (Originality.ai, Graphite, Pangram, Surfer) are vendor estimates with unknown error rates.
- No buyer research exists for the likely purchasers: agencies for ad creative, enterprise IT/L&D for anti-workslop, districts for teaching content.
- No validated portfolio-level diversity metric exists as a success measure for configurations (candidates include Moon et al.'s diversity growth rate and embedding similarity).
- Downstream outcomes (learning gains, engagement, conversion, merge quality) have not been measured for protocol outputs. Validating any configuration end to end would need primary research.
- Cross-cultural effects: AI pulls writers toward Western norms (Agarwal et al.), but there is no evidence on culturally grounded reference sets or seeds.
