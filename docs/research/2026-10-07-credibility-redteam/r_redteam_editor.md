VERDICT: As a PCI RR recommender I would reject this charter at Stage 1 in its current form. That is not a call for a full redesign. The instincts are right: a Registered Report, locked snapshots, a data firewall, open materials and judges kept apart from the loop. Two problems are fatal. (1) The default route is closed to this team. PCI's code of conduct bars authors who have a financial conflict of interest: shares in an entity whose economic interests the results may affect, patents linked to the research, or pay from such an entity. A team building a product on this protocol fits all three. (2) Almost every bias control in the charter is self-certified by the people whose product depends on the result. The team draws the briefs, holds the hash seal and the condition-label keys, runs the batch, owns the ratings database and decides which rating rounds count. Smaller but real gaps: the order of commitments relative to the pilot, tuning effort that is unequal across arms, selection across studies (pilot-shopping, internal evaluations), evidence labels that do not say which way a result went, independence that is only "ideally", measurement instruments the product is allowed to reuse, and an engine whose open release is still undecided. Everything below is fixable for a small team, at a rough cost of $3–8k plus 2–3 engineering weeks. The fixes: move to a journal-based Registered Report that accepts declared conflicts; appoint one independent custodian who holds the briefs, the random seed and the condition map; make the independent statistician and a computational reproducibility certificate mandatory; file a timestamped Stage 0 registration before any pilot rating is unblinded; build a public study log and a fingerprint-keyed claims register. Without these, the research's credibility rests on trusting interested parties. That fails the user's standard of "ABSOLUTELY AND WITHOUT COMPROMISE".

## [fatal] The pre-commitment vehicle the charter names cannot be used. PCI RR's code of conduct says authors 'should have no financial conflicts of interest' and requires every submission to declare 'The authors of this article declare that they have no financial conflict of interest with the content of this article.' PCI defines a financial conflict as pay received in the past four years from an entity whose financial interests the results may affect, shares in such an entity, or patents linked to the research. The authors build a commercial product on the method under test, so they cannot truthfully sign that line. Signing it would be a false declaration, and a product or patent that appears after in-principle acceptance (IPA) would turn the paper into an integrity case. The route the ADR log recommends, PCI RR followed by automatic acceptance at a PCI RR-friendly journal such as RSOS, falls with it. Secondary issue: PCI also forbids AI-generated illustrations, and AI-generated pages are the stimuli shown in figures.
- GAP: CC1 makes PCI RR the default and allows only one fallback trigger, the model-retirement window. CC6 declares the very conflict that makes PCI ineligible, but nothing in the charter checks whether the venue accepts that conflict.
- FIX: Before any Stage 1 writing: (1) Email contact@peercommunityin.org, as PCI's policy invites, describing the exact situation (product, equity, possible patents), and get a written ruling. ## [fatal] Every control is self-custodied by interested parties. The SHA-256 seal on the 48 confirmatory briefs proves the set did not change, not that nobody looked at it, and the team draws and fictionalizes the briefs. 'Blind analysis on scrambled labels' is blind only if someone other than the analysts holds the label key. The ratings land in a Postgres database the team owns, with stimulus IDs the team can map back to arms. The team launches the confirmatory batch and could run it twice and keep one ('never regenerate' is a promise). The team also chooses which Prolific rounds count. PCI's bias-control levels separate proof of non-access (Level 5) from self-certification (Level 4 and below). Under a financial conflict, self-certification is the weakest position available. Reverse leak: sealed confirmatory briefs could be used as product demos or test cases before the run.
- GAP: CC2 (hashes, plus a harness the team controls that refuses unlocked conditions), CC3 (separate stores the team runs) and CC4 (blind analysis the team runs) all rest on team-held keys and team-operated checks. The charter has no independent custodian, no external timestamp and no reconciliation against third-party logs.
- FIX: Appoint one independent custodian: the academic co-investigator if they hold no equity, otherwise the independent statistician. (a) The copywriter delivers confirmatory briefs and copy decks encrypted to the custodian's key. The harness decrypts them only when the locked run launches, and the team does not see them before the data freeze. (b) The brief-draw seed is a future drand beacon round named in the Stage 1. drand is public, verifiable and cannot be biased, so the team cannot re-roll it. (c) The map from stimulus ID to arm is encrypted to the custodian. The Judge database stores opaque IDs only. The custodian releases the map only after the analysis code is frozen and timestamped. (d) Confirmatory generation runs on API keys or workspaces used only for this study, at each provider. The custodian pulls the providers' own usage exports and reconciles call and token counts against the archive, so an undisclosed rerun shows up. Do the same with the Prolific study list for rating rounds. (e) Anchor every lock, data freeze and code freeze with OpenTimestamps and the OSF registration. (f) Disclose honestly what cannot be prevented: stimuli are visually recognizable, so someone who peeks at images can guess arms.

## [major] Pilot results leak into the confirmatory plan. The all-arm pilot (12 development briefs, about 1,500 lay judgments) shows the direction of each contrast before the following are fixed: the smallest effect of interest (SESOI), hypothesis directions, family grouping, the primary test (still unresolved between the two digests, ADR-0037), equivalence margins and AI-judge thresholds. If −D3 looks null, H3d can quietly become an equivalence claim. If CRITIC-self comes out close, it can be moved to exploratory. PCI RR allows pilots but says authors 'should not rely on the outcomes of any preliminary results to establish the utility or overall conclusions'. Reviewers will ask what was known when each decision was made, and the charter cannot answer.
- GAP: The charter does not order commitments relative to the pilot. The only pilot rule (raise briefs to 60 if variance is high) covers sample size, not hypotheses or analysis.

The custodian unblinds the pilot by running a frozen script that releases only nuisance parameters to the team: τ_b, σ_c, σ_r, costs, timings, gold-check pass rates and failure rates. ## [major] Tuning effort is unequal across arms, and product changes can flow back into the study recipe. C1-H will be iterated for months, in the Playground, on the product track and through 'prompt fixes' on development briefs. B-Plain and B-Random are fixed strings and B-Skill is pinned. The contrast then measures a heavily tuned pipeline against untuned prompts, the classic machine-learning baseline problem. Melis et al. found that properly tuned older baselines outperform newer models, and Dodge et al. show that conclusions flip with the tuning budget. Rule 2 says results flow from research to product. Nothing stops prompt changes learned in the product from flowing into the study recipe before the lock, nor limits what counts as a 'fix'. The critic calibration exemplars (2–3 strong sites and 2–3 generic pages) are hand-picked by the team.
- GAP: CC2 freezes prompts at the lock but does not constrain or record how they got there. Rule 2 covers only one direction of flow.
- FIX: At Stage 0, freeze C1-H's content as the article's verbatim prompts plus the declared ⟨A⟩ amendments. On development briefs, allow only failure repairs (build errors, tool misuse, crashes), not taste tuning. Log each repair in the harness with its diff, reason and author, and have an outsider classify each one as 'repair, not tuning'. Give B-Skill and B-Plain the same repair allowance. Report the tuning budget per arm (hours, iterations, tokens), following Dodge et al. An independent designer picks the calibration exemplars under a preregistered rule. A product recipe version may enter a study only through a new Stage 0.
- ENF: process | COST: About 1 day to build the change log, plus ongoing discipline. C1-H may score lower than a tuned version would, and that lower score is the honest estimate.

## [major] Selection across studies: pilot-shopping and internal evaluations. A Registered Report stops selective reporting inside one study, not selection of which studies exist. The product track will keep evaluating configurations and composed recipes with AI judges, blind Playground ratings, small Prolific rounds and user A/B tests. Favourable results will get promoted to registered studies or quoted in marketing; unfavourable ones will quietly disappear. For a product built on 'the right configuration for your problem', this is the most likely route to a biased evidence base. ICMJE requires trials to be registered before enrollment for exactly this reason: to prevent selective publication.
- GAP: CC5 ('publish everything') has no defined denominator. There is no registry of studies or evaluations.
- FIX: Keep a public, append-only study log. Any evaluation that compares configurations, recipes or blocks using human raters or AI judges gets a one-line entry before data collection (question, arms, judge type, N). Its result is posted within 90 days, whatever the outcome. Enforce it in the system: the Judge app refuses to create a Prolific round or an AI-judge batch without a log ID. Product claims may cite only evaluations that were logged and reported, and only confirmatory ones as evidence of efficacy.
- ENF: system | COST: 1–2 days to build the gate, then minutes per study. Some posted nulls will be uncomfortable, which is the intended effect.

## [major] Independence is optional. CC4 says 'ideally' an independent statistician signs off, and does not define independence for the co-investigator, copywriter or experts. If the co-investigator takes equity or an advisory role, their IRB route and sign-off stop being independent. With a financial conflict, analysis by the interested authors is the weakest link. Precedent: in 2005 JAMA began requiring industry-sponsored studies to be analysed independently by statisticians at academic institutions. Wager et al. - GAP: CC4 lists independence measures without making any of them mandatory or defining independence.

Add a CODECHECK certificate (with a DOI) for the analysis pipeline. Write a definition of independence (no equity, no paid role beyond the defined task, no product beta access). The co-investigator, statistician, copywriter and experts each sign it, and the declarations are posted with the materials.

## [major] Outcome-neutral checks, judge-validation thresholds and exclusion rules are missing. Stage 1 reviewers expect outcome-neutral tests fixed in advance, with consequences if they fail (PCI criteria 1E and 2A). The draft has gold pairs and fidelity checks but no pass thresholds. The AI-judge threshold is unspecified ('validated against pilot human judgments'), and methods.md says not to report judges that fail it, which conflicts with the RR rule that every registered analysis is reported. Stimulus-level rules are missing for pages that break the copy deck (CUT may delete REQUIRED strings), text inside generated images, and broken renders. Deciding whether a failure is infrastructure or quality, which decides whether a retry is allowed, is left to human judgment. There is no rule for replacing raters after exclusions. Claesen et al. found deviations concentrated in sample size, exclusions and analysis.
- GAP: The charter has no clause on outcome-neutral criteria, judge validation or exclusions. CC9 says AI judges come from other families but sets no validation standard.
- FIX: Preregister the following. (a) Outcome-neutral thresholds and their consequences: gold-pair accuracy, copy-compliance rate per arm, and fidelity checks (at least one critic round ran in C1-H; seed spread). ## [major] Multiplicity, the decision rule and the equivalence margin. Three Holm families with no control across families, two co-primary outcomes, a lay panel, an expert panel, AI judges and many secondary outcomes leave many routes to a positive headline. The primary test is still undecided. The SESOI (58–60%) and the ±0.08 equivalence margin look chosen to fit power. A ±0.08 margin lets the paper call a block 'negligible' when its true win rate is 57%, and a product team has a cost reason to want that conclusion (D3-auto costs about $2.42 per candidate). Ablation power is 0.81 at 58%. PCI requires a study design table with a prospective interpretation for each outcome, and power based on 'the lowest available or meaningful estimate of the effect size'.
- GAP: The charter never says what counts as 'replication succeeded' or how margins are justified.
- FIX: Choose one primary test now. Use a gatekeeping hierarchy in which H1a (C1-H vs B-Plain, lay quality) is the single gate for the headline claim. ## [major] The product can contaminate the measurement instruments. Rule 4 lets the product reuse the 'sameness check' and defect check as features. If product recipes are tuned against the same similarity measure (Vendi over DreamSim) or tell detector that later studies use as outcomes, the outcome is taught to the test. The protocol already guards against this for D3 by keeping its 4-row tells catalog separate from the Krebs-16 outcome detector. Every new block in the 'lego set' evaluated with those instruments would inherit the contamination. The same goes for judges: if the product uses GPT or Gemini as a quality gate, those families are no longer held out. Same-family judges are biased: LLM evaluators recognize and favour their own generations. Optimizing against the evaluation metric is a form of leakage.
- GAP: CC3 and CC9 firewall data and the loop critic, not evaluation instruments. Rule 4 explicitly permits the product to reuse the checks.
- FIX: Keep an instrument registry on the evaluation side. Any instrument used as an outcome in an active or planned study is marked held out. The product may not import or optimize against it; enforce this with a CI import check in the product code. The product may use only a different implementation, such as a different embedding model or a different detector list. Product quality gates must use a model family that is not a held-out judge in any planned study; if they do use one, that family is retired as a judge.
- ENF: system | COST: Low now: a registry file and a CI rule. The product loses a convenient reuse of the checks.
