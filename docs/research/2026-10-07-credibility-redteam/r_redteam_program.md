# program
VERDICT: Charter v0 (scratchpad/section1_draft.md) guards one paper. It does not guard a program in which the same few people run many studies and also sell a product that gains from favourable results. At program level it has three fatal gaps. (1) There is no denominator: nothing public records that a study or pilot exists before money is spent. The proposed ~$2.2k all-arm pilot, dev-brief prompt tuning, abandoned studies, Paper 2 and "product-informed studies" can all vanish without trace. (2) There is no claims gate: CC7 has no register, no reviewer and no coverage of the site, sales or investor decks, galleries or recommender copy. Meanwhile the product vision (recommend or compose a configuration for any problem) makes almost every product output an untested composite. (3) No one outside the team holds any power: every lock, key, label and publish decision sits with the interested party. Industry bias survives standard risk-of-bias controls (Lundh 2017). TOP 2025's verification practices require an independent party.

Ten more gaps are serious (nine major, one minor):
- Pre-lock tuning is asymmetric: C1 gets prompt fixes while the baselines stay fixed.
- A program can re-test until something wins, and labels follow the latest study.
- Product telemetry can steer which hypotheses get tested.
- "C1" already has five meanings, and rule 2 guarantees the shipped recipe is never the studied one.
- Labels record that a study happened, not what it found.
- Labels never expire, although models retire and drift.
- Locks, timestamps and unblinding are self-attested.
- The ADR log is mutable and unanchored. Verified: the ActAI folder is not a git repository, it lives in OneDrive, and the 37 verifier corrections left no diffs.
- The Registered Report route has an escape clause the team can trigger itself.
- Rule 4 lets the product ship the research instruments, so later outcome measures become circular. COI disclosure is also too narrow (minor).

Most fixes are cheap for a small team because the architecture already has the right choke points. The model gateway is the sole egress with spend caps, and the engine has a recipe registry, a content-addressed archive and a separate evaluation side. Make the gateway refuse spend without a public registry ID. Compute labels from the registry by hash. Anchor locks and manifests in OSF/PCI RR and Rekor. Escrow the unblinding key with an external monitor. Lint claims in CI. Put the decision log under git with Zenodo snapshots. The parts that cost real money or time are an independent Integrity Monitor (my estimate: $3–8k a year), mandatory PCI RR lead time (2–4 months per study, my estimate) and a standing replication budget for label renewal. These are the price of "ABSOLUTELY AND WITHOUT COMPROMISE".

## [fatal] Program-level file drawer. Pilots, dev-brief runs, abandoned studies and non-RR studies can disappear without trace, so the published record shows only the studies the company chose to finish.
- GAP: CC5 ('publish everything') and CC1 work per study, and only after a study reaches Stage 1 or produces a result. Nothing records that a study or pilot exists before spend. The open decision to run an all-arm pilot (~$2.2k generation + ~$0.6k raters on 12 dev briefs) happens before any registration. If C1 looks weak there, the team can simply never file Stage 1, and no one can tell. PCI RR's public withdrawal record (§3.12) protects only studies that reached in-principle acceptance. OSF lets a registration stay embargoed for up to 4 years. Paper 2 and 'product-informed studies' have no registration duty. Franco et al. found that null results mostly die because authors never write them up, not because journals reject them.
- FIX: Create a public Program Registry: one OSF project plus a registry/ folder in the public git repo. Every study and every pilot gets an entry before its first paid call, with ID, question, arms, recipe hashes, phase (dev, pilot or confirmatory), planned dates and a results-due date. Lifecycle: planned, piloting, locked, collecting, analysing, then reported or abandoned. Rules: (a) post full results, nulls included, to the entry within 12 months of the last data (the WHO standard); (b) an abandoned entry needs a dated reason plus release of whatever data and code exist; (c) design details may be embargoed for at most 12 months, and an entry's existence never; (d) every paper and every product claim reports the program denominator: how many studies, pilots and arms tested each recipe or block, and what they found. The registry is append-only (git plus Zenodo snapshots, see the decision-log attack).
- ENF: system | COST: About 2 dev days to make the gateway (ADR-0030, sole egress with spend caps) refuse any call that lacks a registry ID with a live public entry whose status permits that phase (dev, pilot or confirmatory). Add a nightly job that sends overdue entries to the Integrity Monitor, who publishes the list. About 30 minutes per study to file. No cash. The real cost is that plans become visible to competitors, mitigated by the 12-month design-detail embargo.
- SRC: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25170047; https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/ampps/25152459211007467/; https://rr.peercommunityin.org/about/full_policies; https://www.who.int/news/item/09-04-2015-japan-primary-registries-network; https://help.osf.io/article/330-welcome-to-registrations; https://www.cos.io/initiatives/top-guidelines

## [fatal] Product claims outrun the evidence through marketing copy, cherry-picked showcase galleries, sales and investor decks, and recommender UI. Typical risks: implying 'research-backed' for untested composite recipes, or quoting C1-H landing-page results for fiction, decks or code.
- GAP: CC7 says 'claims only what published evidence supports' but has no claims register, no reviewer, no expiry, and no coverage of non-paper channels. 'Published' includes the team's own preprints. Under the product vision, a recommender or composer builds configurations for any problem from the lego set, so most product outputs are untested composites. The only evidence in view is C1-H on landing pages: one worker family, static desktop renders, lay preference raters. Hand-picked galleries from many runs imply more than the averages show. Lundh et al.: industry-sponsored studies reach favourable conclusions more often, and their conclusions agree less with their results. The FTC's Workado order shows an AI-product claim stretched beyond what the evidence covered is actionable.
- FIX: Keep a Claims Register. Any public statement that references research, effectiveness, comparison or quality ('research-backed', 'proven', 'outperforms', 'less generic', '% better') must carry a claim ID. The ID resolves to registry result(s) with effect size, CI, recipe hash, model IDs, domain, rater population and expiry. No claim may cite a study before its full results, nulls included, are public in the registry. Showcases are seeded random draws from archived runs, or must state the selection ratio (e.g. '3 hand-picked of 40'). The recommender shows the recommended recipe's evidence label and says 'routing untested' until the recommender itself has been studied. Corrections go out within 14 days of a valid complaint and are logged publicly.
- ENF: process | COST: About 2 dev days. (1) A CI linter on the site and docs repos fails any build containing claim-pattern phrases without a valid, unexpired claim ID. (2) A gallery sampler script is the only way to build showcase pages. Plus about 1 hour per marketing release for Integrity Monitor sign-off on research-referencing claims, and a quarterly public claims audit. Decks and talks are outside CI, so the same sign-off covers them. Strategic cost: weaker copy until the evidence exists. It also matches the FTC requirement for competent and reliable evidence at the time a claim is made.
- SRC: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation; https://www.ftc.gov/node/297697; https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.2; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28207928; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36037471

## [fatal] No party outside the team holds any power. Every lock, unblinding key, label, claim and publish-or-not decision sits with the people who profit. If the company stalls, pivots or folds, nobody can force registered results into the open.
- GAP: CC4 limits independence to materials (copywriter, expert raters) and says only 'ideally an independent statistician'. The academic co-investigator becomes a co-author, and Lakens notes that making a critic a co-author is itself a conflict of interest. ICMJE requires authors to be free to publish 'when and where they choose', but here sponsor and authors are the same people. TOP 2025's two verification practices, results transparency and computational reproducibility, require 'a party independent from the researchers'. Lundh: industry bias 'cannot be explained by standard Risk of bias assessments'. Self-policed controls are exactly what fails.
- FIX: Appoint a standing Integrity Monitor: one methodologist on no author list, paid a fixed fee not contingent on results, with the co-investigator as second signatory. A written charter, itself registered and changed only by a new public version, gives the Monitor these powers: (1) holds the unblinding key; (2) checks the report against the registration and reruns the analysis from the data freeze before submission; (3) signs off research-referencing claims and evidence-label changes; (4) has read access to the registry, archive and gateway ledger; (5) holds a contractual right to post registered results and a dissent to the registry if the team misses a results-due date (results escrow that survives company failure); (6) publishes an annual integrity report. Add a paid red-team review of each Stage 1 protocol.
- ENF: external_party | COST: My estimate, unverified: $3–8k per year honorarium plus about 2 hours a month of team time. A paid red-team review costs about a week of one reviewer per study. A cheaper floor that still counts: PCI RR recommender plus a red-team bounty per study. It cannot be skipped if 'without compromise' holds, because every other safeguard relies on it.
- SRC: https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/author-responsibilities--conflicts-of-interest.html; https://www.cos.io/initiatives/top-guidelines; https://media.nature.com/original/magazine-assets/d41586-020-01392-8/d41586-020-01392-8.pdf; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28207928

## [major] Unequal tuning before the lock. The authors iterate on their own recipe (C1 prompts, critic calibration) during the pilot until it looks good, while baselines stay as fixed strings. A study can be won before it is locked.
- GAP: CC2 locks only after the pilot, and nothing governs the time before it. study1-draft allows 'prompt fixes only on development briefs' plus one critic calibration, with no cap on iterations and no equal-effort rule. B-Plain and B-Random are fixed strings and B-Skill is a pinned commit. Sealed confirmatory briefs prevent overfitting to the briefs, not to the method. Dodge et al. show that model comparisons flip with tuning budget. The Leaderboard Illusion documents private testing of many variants (27 for one release) followed by selective disclosure.
- FIX: Register the pilot (see the file-drawer attack) with: what may be tuned in each arm; an equal tuning budget per arm in iterations and dollars, or a matched budget for a non-author prompt-tuner working on the baselines; a go/no-go rule; and at most one pilot round. A second round is a new registry entry. Archive every prompt and recipe version touched in the pilot, with timestamps, and ship the diff history in the replication package. The paper reports per-arm tuning budget and, where feasible, expected performance against budget.
- ENF: system | COST: About 1 dev day: the gateway tags every dev-brief call by arm and recipe hash and enforces per-arm caps, and Lab refuses to lock a plan unless the per-arm tuning table is generated from the gateway ledger. A non-author baseline tuner costs a few hundred dollars (estimate). PCI RR already requires pilots to be published in the Stage 2 manuscript.
- SRC: https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03004; https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879; https://rr.peercommunityin.org/about/full_policies

## [major] Re-testing until one works across studies. Each study is preregistered, but the program re-runs C1 or a block on a new model, critic mode or domain and leads with whichever run wins. Labels follow the latest or best study.
- GAP: CC7 lets any single study make a recipe 'Studied'. Nothing aggregates evidence across studies or forces a null from study k to stay next to a positive from study k+1. Likely flashpoints: a null −CRITIC ablation that the product depends on, or a weak C1-H effect re-tested on 'GREAT-mode CRITIC' or a different worker.
- FIX: Derive labels and claims from the cumulative registered evidence per recipe-hash family and per block: a living per-claim evidence table listing all registered results, effects and CIs, with a pooled estimate where designs are comparable. A re-test of anything that came out null or inconclusive must be registered as a replication citing the prior result, and both stay visible. Put planned multi-study lines (Paper 1 → Paper 2) into a PCI RR programmatic RR, which fixes the components and their Stage 2 outputs up front. Parts can be withdrawn only publicly.
- ENF: system | COST: About 2 dev days for the evidence table and the label computation, which reads every registry result for the hash family, with no manual override. A programmatic RR needs more Stage 1 scoping but avoids repeated Stage 1 cycles.
- SRC: https://rr.peercommunityin.org/about/full_policies; https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25170047

## [major] Product telemetry steers what gets tested. Domains, brief sampling frames, comparators and rater populations get chosen where usage data says C1 wins, so even perfectly preregistered studies sample a flattering slice. Product A/B tests become an unregistered evidence stream for marketing.
- GAP: CC3 and design rule 3 only keep product data out of confirmatory analyses. They do not govern hypothesis selection, comparator choice, sampling frames or which studies get funded. Lundh: industry bias lives outside standard risk-of-bias items, which points to choices like these. Product A/B experiments are not covered by any charter item. Berman et al. found that most commercial A/B experimenters stop when a positive effect reaches 90% confidence, raising the false-discovery rate.
- FIX: (a) Every preregistration carries a telemetry-exposure statement modelled on PCI RR's bias-control levels: what product data on this domain the authors had seen, auto-filled from the data firewall's access logs. (b) Register the program's question roadmap (the ADR-0024 ladder) now, before telemetry exists; later additions record their provenance. (c) Every study preregisters at least one outcome the product could lose on (cost, defects, comprehension or conversion) and includes the strongest available competitor baseline, picked by the Integrity Monitor. (d) Product A/B results may be cited publicly only if registered before launch with a fixed horizon or sequential-valid statistics. Otherwise they stay internal.
- ENF: disclosure | COST: About 1 day to add per-user query logging to the research/product data firewall; about 1 hour per preregistration; the Monitor picks a baseline once per study. Product cost: some A/B wins can't be marketed.
- SRC: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28207928; https://rr.peercommunityin.org/about/full_policies; https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/what-marketers-are-doing-wrong-in-data-analytics/

## [major] Recipe drift. 'C1' comes to mean something different over time, and the product sells a name whose evidence belongs to a different, retired version.
- GAP: 'C1' already has five meanings: the article's workflow, protocol C1 with ten ⟨A⟩ amendments, C1-H (ADR-0034), r_methods' C1-auto, and the lite profile. Design rule 2 ('results flow research → product via new recipe versions') guarantees the shipped version is never the studied one. ADR-0038 replaces the article's models with current ones. CC2 pins versions only inside a study; nothing binds the public name to the studied hash. Behaviour drifts even under a fixed model name: Chen et al. measured GPT-4 prime identification falling from 84% to 51% in three months.
- FIX: Public identifiers are name@version+hash; the paper's arm becomes, for example, 'C1-H v1.0.0 (hash) on claude-opus-5-5 worker + claude-fable-5-1 critic'. Breaking-change policy: a change to block wiring, defaults, prompts, worker or critic model, or checkpoint binding means a new version, and a change to topology means a new name (extend ADR-0019). 'Studied' attaches to an exact hash. A derived version reads 'Derived from studied X: untested' until a registered bridging study re-earns the label. Per the study1 cost model, a C1-H vs B-Plain bridge on 24 briefs costs about $2k generation plus about $0.6k raters. Papers describe recipes with TIDieR items, including 'modifications'.
- ENF: system | COST: 3–5 dev days, since the draft already plans a recipe registry. The product UI and docs render name and label only by hash lookup, and release CI blocks any product release whose recipe claims a label its hash did not earn. Bridging studies run about $2–4k each, when the team wants a label back.
- SRC: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24609605; https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009

## [major] Evidence labels can be gamed. They record that a study happened, not what it found or where it applies, and composite recipes can borrow their blocks' labels.
- GAP: CC7's labels (Studied / Research-grounded but untested / Composed-experimental) are self-assigned, per recipe, with no direction, scope or certainty. A recipe whose ablations were null, or C1-H that beat only B-Plain on lay preference, still reads 'Studied' to a buyer. 'Research-grounded' is a euphemism any recipe can earn by citing a paper. The scheme is a third vocabulary next to the protocol README's (Evidence-backed / Mixed / Article prescription / ⟨A⟩) and the ADR provenance labels.
- FIX: Labels are per claim and computed: Supported (registered, CI excludes no effect, scope stated), No meaningful effect (TOST-equivalent), Inconclusive, Worse, or Untested. Each label carries GRADE-style downgrades for indirectness: different model, domain, rater population, outcome (lay preference is not conversion) or checkpoint mode (headless vs human-in-the-loop). Composites never inherit: a composed recipe is Untested until studied as a whole. One vocabulary across protocol, papers and product, with a published mapping table.
- ENF: system | COST: About 2 dev days for computation from the registry; only the Integrity Monitor can approve a label change, and every change is logged. Some editorial work to unify the three vocabularies.
- SRC: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21802903; https://www.ftc.gov/node/297697; https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation

## [major] Evidence never expires. A 2027 result on models that later retire keeps backing 2029 claims about a product running different models.
- GAP: No charter item retires a label or a claim. ADR-0038 already deals with retirements (Sonnet 4.5 on 2026-11-30, Haiku 4.5 from 2026-10-15) and model drift. The protocol's own default rule 10 ('Bank what failed, and retest it when new models ship') is not applied to the team's own claims.
- FIX: Every Supported or No-effect label expires at the earlier of the studied model's retirement date or 24 months after the data freeze. On expiry it degrades automatically to 'Historical: studied on <model>, retired', and every claim citing it expires too (caught by the claims linter). Renewal only through a registered replication or bridging study. Publish the sunset schedule in the registry.
- ENF: system | COST: Under 1 dev day for the scheduler. Ongoing: a standing replication budget the team must plan for, from a bridge (~$2–4k) up to a full rerun (~$20–24k, the study1 core and lean estimates).
- SRC: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21802903

## [major] Locks, timestamps, deviations and unblinding are self-attested. The team can re-lock, reorder or peek, and declare only the deviations it chooses.
- GAP: CC2's 'harness refuses any condition not in the lock' is enforced by code the team writes and can re-lock. Run times sit in the team's own Postgres and Temporal. CC4's blind analysis on scrambled labels means nothing if the same people hold the key. CC8 relies on authors declaring deviations: Claesen et al. found 9 of 27 preregistered studies disclosed none of their deviations, and COMPare found widespread outcome misreporting even in CONSORT-endorsing journals. On the CC1 fallback route, no third party sees the plan before the data.
- FIX: (a) A lock is valid only when its hash appears in a public, frozen registration (PCI RR Stage 1 or OSF) dated before the first confirmatory call. The harness fetches the registration via API and checks hash and date before starting. (b) A daily Merkle root of run manifests, rating events and the gateway ledger goes to a public transparency log (Sigstore Rekor), so ordering and timing are anchored outside the team. (c) The condition-label key is encrypted to the Integrity Monitor and released only after the analysis-code commit hash is registered. (d) The deviation table is generated by diffing executed manifests against the lock and included verbatim. Under PCI RR §3.10, any change after in-principle acceptance is cleared with the recommender before data collection ends.
- ENF: system | COST: About 3–4 dev days in total; no cash; about 1 hour of Monitor time per study to release the key.
- SRC: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34729209; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30760329; https://rr.peercommunityin.org/about/full_policies; https://docs.sigstore.dev/logging/overview/; https://help.osf.io/article/330-welcome-to-registrations

## [major] The Registered Report escape hatch is triggered by the team itself. Choosing a model or timeline that 'breaks the retirement window' lets a study skip in-principle acceptance, and with it the publication guarantee for nulls.
- GAP: CC1 makes PCI RR the default, with an OSF fallback 'if the RR timeline breaks the model-retirement window'. The team controls that condition through model choice, which ADR-0038 leaves open. Without in-principle acceptance, the positive-result filter returns: 96% positive results in standard reports vs 44% in RRs. OSF registrations can stay embargoed for up to four years.
- FIX: Any confirmatory study that a product claim may ever cite goes through PCI RR, as a programmatic RR for the Paper 1/Paper 2 line where it fits. The fallback requires the Integrity Monitor's written sign-off recorded in the registry and an immediately public OSF registration with no embargo, and the resulting label is capped at a visible 'Supported (non-RR)'. Choose models whose retirement dates leave room for Stage 1 review; ADR-0038's six-month rule already points that way.
- ENF: process | COST: About 2–4 months of Stage 1 lead time per study (my estimate) plus revision effort. No engineering.

## [major] The product absorbs the research instruments, so later studies become circular. Recipes tuned in the product against the sameness, diversity, tell or defect checks are later 'evaluated' with those same checks.
- GAP: Design rule 4 lets the product reuse 'cheap automated checks' (sameness check, defect check) as features. The protocol forbids the loop's critic from judging success (ADR-0015), and methods.md calls scoring a treatment with the instrument it optimises against circular. Nothing extends that rule across the program over time. The Leaderboard Illusion shows that access to an evaluation's own data produces large gains on that distribution (up to 112% relative).
- FIX: Keep an instrument-exposure register. Every evaluation instrument (embedding model, tell detector, judge prompt, defect checklist, brief set) has a hash. Once an instrument ships as a product feature or an in-loop gate, recipes released after that date are flagged 'exposed', and that instrument may serve only as an exploratory outcome for them. Maintain a held-out set that the product never ships: held-out tells list, judge prompts and sealed briefs.
- ENF: system | COST: About 1–2 dev days: the evaluation side checks recipe lineage against the register before computing confirmatory metrics. Product cost: separate instrument versions, or a few features given up.
- SRC: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879

## [minor] COI disclosure is too narrow. It covers papers only and omits financial magnitude, the company's role, intellectual allegiance and AI authorship of the design.
- GAP: CC6's statement ('authors build a product on this protocol') appears only in papers. It omits: equity or revenue tied to the recipes; the company's role in design, conduct, analysis and the decision to publish; allegiance to a protocol the authors wrote; and that an AI model from the same family as the worker and critic drafted the protocol, study designs and ADRs. Talks, blog posts, dataset cards and the product's research page are not covered. ICMJE counts 'intellectual beliefs' as a potential conflict, and CONSORT 2025 items 5a and 5b require the funder's role and authors' financial and other conflicts.
- FIX: Every author files an ICMJE-style disclosure. A standing public COI page in the registry is linked from every output (paper, preprint, dataset card, talk, product research page). It states the company's role in design, conduct, analysis and the decision to publish, and discloses AI assistance in protocol and study design.
- ENF: disclosure | COST: About 2 hours once, minutes per output.
