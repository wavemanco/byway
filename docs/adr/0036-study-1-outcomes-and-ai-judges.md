## Context

C1 claims two things: its pages look better, and they look less generic. Lay raters tend to prefer prototypical layouts. Every arm is generated by Claude, and C1-H is optimized against a Claude critic, so a Claude judge would favour its own family's output and reward the loop's own target. Published tests put general VLM judges near chance on subtle UI differences. The accepted judges decision (ADR 23) leaves the mix open.

## Decision

Proposed, not decided.

Primary outcomes: two lay pairwise forced choices collected on Prolific, each from a separate rater pool so one judgment doesn't colour the other.
- Quality: 'Which page looks better designed?'
- Distinctiveness: 'Which page looks more distinctive — less like other websites you've seen?'
Raters see static full-page desktop renders side by side with synced scroll. Sides are randomized and counterbalanced. Sessions include gold/check pairs, and exclusions are preregistered.

Secondary outcomes:
- an expert designer panel;
- diversity metrics (Vendi over DreamSim);
- a deterministic tell detector independent of the TELLS catalog;
- a 5-second comprehension test;
- defect and accessibility guardrails;
- cost.

AI judges are secondary only:
- At least two non-Claude families, e.g. GPT-5.6 Sol and Gemini 3.1 Pro; r_measures adds an open-weight VLM.
- Never the loop critic, and Claude is never in the headline panel. The digests disagree on any other use of Claude: r_measures runs a Claude judge separately as a self-preference probe, while r_study1 says never to use any Claude model as a judge.
- They see the same renders as humans, use frozen prompts that differ from the critic's, and judge every pair in both orders.
- Each judge is validated against pilot human judgments before its results are reported. Disagreement with humans is reported as a finding.

Per port rule 8, no outcome uses an AI detector.

## Alternatives considered

- **Left / Can't decide / Right, with a tie option (r_ux)** — Lets raters mark close pairs and makes identical-pair traps possible, but some raters overuse ties. r_methods specifies 2AFC with no tie button.
- **r_methods wording: Pool A 'Which would you rather use / which do you prefer?'; Pool B 'Which looks less generic / template-like?', with distinctiveness gated behind H1** — Matches a 'preferred' claim directly, and the gate protects the main hypothesis. It frames distinctiveness as genericness and makes it secondary in order.
- **Absolute Likert ratings, or Best-Worst Scaling of 4-tuples** — Likert gives absolute levels but has scale-use noise. BWS is more reliable per annotation but heavy with full-page screenshots.
- **Embedding diversity (DreamSim-Vendi on text-masked renders) as a co-primary outcome (r_measures)** — Cheap, continuous and set-level. Not yet validated on UIs, so it needs a preregistered fallback to human-rated distinctiveness.
- **Expert panel or AI judges as the primary outcome** — Experts give construct validity but are expensive. AI judges are cheap but near chance on subtle UI contrasts and circular with the VLM critic.
- **All frontier families as judges, including Claude** — Strongest individual judges, but the design would reward the critic's own taste.
- **Prediction-powered inference: AI judges label all pairs, humans a random subset** — Valid confidence intervals whatever the judge's quality, and buys power cheaply. Small gains if judges are near chance.
- **Live sandboxed iframes instead of static renders** — More realistic and shows motion. Varies by device, is harder to archive, and AI judges would see something different.

## Consequences

**Positive**

- Is one concrete mix within the accepted decision to support all three judge types (ADR 23).
- Avoids self-preference, because the headline judges are not from the family that generated and critiqued the pages.

**Negative**

- Lay preference for prototypical layouts may bias quality judgments against distinctive designs.
- Two co-primary outcomes cost statistical power.
- AI judges may add little if they are near chance on these contrasts.

**Follow-ups**

- User to decide the exact question wording and the tie policy.
- Decide whether a Claude judge may run as a separate self-preference probe (r_measures) or not at all (r_study1).
- Set rater pay ($13.50/h vs $15/h in the digests, both above Prolific's $12/h guidance) and rater geography.
- The Judge rater app must implement synced scroll, scroll-depth gating and check pairs.
- Split human labels into a calibration set and a sealed validation set for the AI judges.
