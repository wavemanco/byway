- **Severity:** should-fix; **refuters:** confirmed; **where:** `packages/gateway/src/providers/fal.ts`

### R-MONEY-2: A resumed clip past its wait now waits as long as its one status read takes; the records say it is cancelled 'at once'

- **Severity:** note; **where:** `packages/gateway/src/providers/fal.ts`
- **What:** Before MONEY-2's fix, a resumed job past its wait was cancelled at once, wrongly, because of its 0 ms deadline. Now pollJob's `deadline.passed` path waits on seenDone, raced only against the stop. seenDone has no byway bound: req.attempt retries it as a free read (DEFAULT_RETRY maxAttempts 4), and each try is bounded only by Node fetch's limits, about 305 s per the repo's own probe. The second look after a failed cancel is the same, and is not raced even against the stop. ADR-0114 item 12 says a job 'queued, running or unreadable is cancelled at once, as before'. That holds only when the read fails fast; a hung read is waited out. No money is miscounted: no clip is bought twice, and the cancel still comes. The builder disclosed this in their handoff, but no record or row carries it.
- **Evidence:** Probe e in the same probe file and log (paths under R-MONEY-1). A resumed job is accepted at T0, resumed at T0+45 min, and fal's status read takes 12 minutes to say IN_PROGRESS. At 12 min - 1 ms the call is not over and no cancel has been sent. The cancel goes at +12 min, and the call ends 'waited' at 0.5 (["status req-earlier","cancel req-earlier"], cancelAt+min [12]). Code: fal.ts seenDone (`req.attempt(() => queue.status(...))`, no Deadline), pollJob `untilEnded(seenDone(...), stop)` and waitedGeneration's `seenDone(req, req.model, ...)`. retry.ts: DEFAULT_RETRY.maxAttempts 4, FREE_READ.
- **Proposed fix:** Bound seenDone with a Deadline of its own (RESULT_LOOK_MS or CANCEL_TIMEOUT_MS, 30 s) and read a read that runs out as unreadable, so the job is cancelled. Race the second look against the stop as well. Or keep the code and make ADR-0114 item 12 say the status read is waited for up to its retries and Node's fetch limits.
